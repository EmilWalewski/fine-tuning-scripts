Dom Development S.A. Group’s 2025 consolidated sustainability reporting identified, through double materiality, 4 material consumer and end-user topics: privacy, access to high-quality information, access to products and services, and responsible marketing practices. The real estate and construction company addresses these impacts through policies, procedures and standards. Privacy is managed through personal data protection procedures designed to prevent confidentiality breaches and protect individual rights during contract signing. Access to information is supported by clear, legally compliant contractual provisions and communication rules aligned with anti-greenwashing principles, strengthening transparency and reliability. The Group’s activity increases housing availability, responds to needs of major agglomerations and supports improvement of the housing situation. Responsible marketing emphasizes reliable, verified information for clients, investors and shareholders, building trust and transparency. In 2025, the Group initiated plain language in documentation, including customer contracts, and external communication. It set no targets or metrics for this topic and has no performance results.

Under G1-1 corporate culture and business conduct, the Group has several policies covering business conduct and corporate culture but conducts no internal training in this area. Group companies established corporate culture through defined values, mission and vision communicated to employees, and develop it through internal communication, including management board messages, newsletters and intranet, training implementation, and support for integration and employee initiatives. Corporate culture is assessed by employee feedback and turnover indicator analysis.

The Code of Ethical Conduct sets ethical conduct guidelines and rules for all persons connected with the company. It defines core values: respect for human rights, integrity, respect, responsibility, anti-discrimination, transparency and anti-corruption. It covers violation-reporting mechanisms, including anonymous reporting and whistleblower protection. The Code covers ethical principles inside the company, including working conditions, occupational health and safety, and professional development, and external relations with clients, contractors and the market, emphasizing professionalism, transparency and sustainable development. It serves as an internal guide for ethical culture and legal compliance.

For whistleblower protection, selected Group companies created a system for reporting irregularities, crimes and abuses, aligned with legal requirements and openness. The system is described in publicly available Procedures for Reporting Abuses and Protecting Whistleblowers, compliant with relevant rules, including external reporting. The procedures permit reporting of all irregularities, including corruption, and define obligations of the reporter and Group company. Reports may be made through indicated channels, including anonymously in described cases, via procedural channels and dedicated e-mails: etyka@domd.pl, etykadc@domd.pl, etykaddsa@domd.pl, etykaddw@domd.pl, etykaddkw@domd.pl, etykaddk@domd.pl and etykaES@eurostyl.com.pl; traditional correspondence; telephone; or in person. Suspicions may also be disclosed openly to the direct supervisor through the service path, in special cases directly to the president of the management board, or to the risk analysis and compliance team via riskandcompliance@domd.pl or riskandcompliance@eurostyl.com.pl, e-mail, telephone or another common company method. The Group Risk and Compliance Director is available to personnel in every situation. Employees have permanent intranet access to procedure materials. In 2025, no training was held for employees or report recipients. Any information, regardless of channel, starts an internal explanatory proceeding. Good-faith reporting excludes retaliation. The company does not seek to identify anonymous reporters. For official service-path reports, Group companies provide support and protect the reporter’s identity. The Group has no policy on internal business conduct training.

The Anti-Corruption, Gifts and Conflicts of Interest Policy develops the principle of total rejection and condemnation of all actions showing signs of corruption in current business operations of Group companies. Its objectives are corruption risk minimization, reducing the probability of corrupt practices among persons involved in business cooperation; compliance risk management as a due-diligence instrument; an integrity requirement for all persons involved in business cooperation to act honestly and legally; and conflict-of-interest prevention. The Policy regulates anti-corruption rules for personnel and business partners, rules for accepting and giving gifts to minimize corruption risk, and identification of corruption threats, competition protection and conflict-of-interest management. It does not directly refer to the United Nations Convention against Corruption. The Group will prepare an update no earlier than 2030. Group companies have procedures for fast, independent and objective investigation of business conduct incidents, including corruption and bribery. Functions most exposed to corruption and bribery risk are offering and contracting, relations with offices and public institutions, supply chain and subcontractor cooperation, and financial settlements.
