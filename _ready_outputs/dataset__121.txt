Dom Development S.A. Group 2025 sustainability reporting defines the CapEx KPI denominator as additions to property, plant and equipment, increases in right-of-use assets from note 7.7 of the 2025 consolidated financial statements, and internally generated or acquired intangible assets from note 7.6. For 2025 taxonomy disclosures, Group companies analysed activities and identified taxonomy-eligible activities; disclosed financial data cover eligible turnover and related CapEx/OpEx. The Group’s core activity is construction of residential buildings. Eligible activities identified: CCM 7.1 construction of new buildings, including residential and non-residential projects using financial, technical and physical resources for later sale and complete buildings on own account for sale or under remuneration/contract; CCM 7.7 acquisition and ownership of buildings, covering real estate purchase and ownership rights; BIO 2.1 hotels, holiday houses, campsites and similar facilities, covering short-term tourist accommodation with services; CCM 4.15 district heating/cooling distribution, covering construction, modernisation and operation of heat/cooling networks ending at a substation or heat exchanger; CCM 5.1 construction, extension and operation of water collection, treatment and supply systems; CCM 6.5 motorcycles, passenger cars and light commercial vehicles, covering purchase, financing, rental, leasing and operation of M1 vehicles. A taxonomy-eligible activity is environmentally sustainable only if it substantially contributes to an environmental objective, does no significant harm, and complies with Article 18 minimum safeguards under Regulation 2020/852. The Group assessed eligible activities against Delegated Regulations 2021/2139, 2023/2485 and 2023/2486 and found no activity fully meeting all technical screening criteria. Causes: buildings did not meet energy-efficiency standards of at least 10% below the nearly zero-energy building threshold, projects lacked life-cycle global warming potential calculations, and documentation was insufficient to confirm taxonomy compliance unequivocally. The Group assessed EU Taxonomy minimum safeguards and found adopted policies and procedures sufficient for minimum social safeguards; it excluded indications of failure in any reviewed area, and no breaches or irregularities ending in final judgments or decisions were identified. The review followed the Final Report on Minimum Safeguards by the Platform on Sustainable Finance. The Group states that it meets minimum safeguards by operating in line with OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises and UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights. OECD disclosure areas referenced are human rights, employment and employee relations, environment, anti-bribery and other corruption, consumer interests, competition and taxation. The Group acts transparently, discloses activity information, and publishes material matters potentially affecting enterprise value on its investor website. Dom Development Group declares respect for human rights across the value chain. The Code of Ethical Conduct, Fundamental Rights Policy, Code of Conduct and whistleblowing procedure aim to prevent violations. The Group respects human-rights and children’s-rights laws, rejects modern slavery, human-rights violations and labour-rights violations, and commits to international humanitarian-law standards and humanitarian principles in crisis or conflict situations. The Fundamental Rights Policy sets objectives, principles and frameworks for high ethical and legal standards, human-rights observance and equal employment opportunities regardless of sex, race, age, religion or origin, while protecting minorities, women, children, persons with disabilities, migrant workers and their families. The Group opposes forced labour and child labour in own operations and imposes related obligations on counterparties. Group companies commit to fundamental-rights due diligence across the value chain, identification of violation risks, identification of high-risk counterparties, agreements with fundamental-rights clauses requiring policy compliance, and counterparty monitoring. The Code of Conduct is included in counterparty agreements. Counterparties must observe international humanitarian-law standards, including human-rights and children’s-rights rules, and inform Dom Development of potential human-rights or humanitarian-principle violation risks during cooperation. The Group maintains violation reporting channels and reviews and, where needed, updates these documents.
