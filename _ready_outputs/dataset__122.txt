XTB S.A. Group 2025 consolidated annual report excerpt identifies regulatory, operational, legal, compliance, cybersecurity, tax, HR, and conduct risks with controls. Market abuse risk covers XTB holding financial instruments affected by manipulation, or derivatives based on such instruments, and use of XTB accounts for manipulation; potential outcomes include own-account losses and regulatory liability if the Company fails to respond appropriately under MAR; mitigation: implemented MAR abuse-detection system. AML risk concerns incorrect or insufficient procedures and measures for client identification, client transaction monitoring, and monitoring of business relationships; consequences include high financial penalties, risk of revocation of brokerage licences, use of XTB infrastructure for money laundering, termination of relationships by correspondent banks and payment processors, paralysis of deposit acceptance and withdrawal execution, public accusations of facilitating financial crime, permanent reputational damage, and rapid outflow of investor capital; mitigants: AML procedures, financial security measures, internal controls, and internal audits. Information leakage, unauthorized disclosure or use of inside information, illegal use of client data or other legally protected information, and unauthorized data access may cause high administrative fines, costly legal disputes, lasting loss of reputation and shareholder trust, identity theft, unauthorized transactions/ATO, and loss of financial-data privacy for users; protections include XTB ICT environment safeguards, external certified IT-security providers, confidential-information flow procedures, security-incident management, MAR market-abuse detection, documented employee investment rules, restricted lists, access-control mechanisms for premises and IT systems, insider lists, and employee awareness training. Financial-market regulatory change and failure to understand or comply with selected regulatory requirements create high operating adaptation costs, financial sanctions, and loss of licences in key jurisdictions; new rules may restrict access to selected instruments for defined client groups, including leverage limits, and alter protection standards, affecting trading conditions and service scope; mitigation: regulatory change monitoring and early implementation planning. Local branch-law application risk reflects the need to adapt processes to multiple supervisory regimes, increasing operational complexity and penalties for non-compliance with local reporting, capital, or outsourcing requirements; local legal differences shape investor protection, marketing communication standards, and complaint-handling procedures; response: remedial actions or compliance control over adopted solutions. Misinterpretation of regulations may cause operational and internal-process errors, regulatory-reporting mistakes, capital-adequacy ratio calculation errors, and costs of projects that cannot be finalized for regulatory reasons; in a multi-jurisdictional regime it exposes XTB to financial sanctions, legal risk, possible restrictions on licensed activity, inadequate product selection for investment profiles, weakened protection standards under local regulations, erosion of trust in broker transparency, and potential intensified supervisory inspections; controls include Legal and Compliance Department support for relevant departments, use of external independent law firms for material issues, and internal controls. Unauthorized or illegal marketing or promotional communication, incorrect labelling of commercial and promotional information, erroneous advertising messages, misleading clients, or unauthorized advice may trigger sanctions from multiple supervisors, campaign-withdrawal orders, higher customer acquisition costs from reduced brand credibility, client misunderstanding of product characteristics and risks, unsuitable investment decisions, and financial losses; mitigation: strict compliance with regulations, best marketing practices, XTB internal procedures, control over publication of commercial materials, and employment of local legal advertising and marketing specialists. Improper fulfilment of client information duties exposes XTB to severe financial penalties from KNF, FCA, and CySEC, legal claims, reduced growth and profitability, client losses from non-transparent costs and risks, wrong decisions, and potential client outflow to competitors; controls: internal controls, Compliance controls, and internal procedures. Improper designation of target markets and application of laws related to acquiring and classifying clients may cause financial penalties, supervisory sanctions, materially slower client-base growth due to erroneous profiling, client losses on instruments unsuitable to knowledge level, and loss of protections attached to specific investor classes; mitigants: product-introduction procedures requiring preliminary agreement and Legal and Compliance approval of the target group for each new product, plus regular product reviews to identify events materially affecting core product features and the accepted target market. Incorrect tax calculation, incorrect tax settlements, and tax-return errors create financial and fiscal-penal sanctions, labour-intensive reporting corrections, capital and operational-resource burden, and undermined credibility of XTB as a public-trust institution, potentially causing negative share valuation by shareholders; controls: double checking, audit, internal control processes, and local tax advisers. Staffing shortages, particularly single-person positions, may disrupt process continuity, slow task execution, create failure to meet supervisory expectations for legally required actions, and, in small units or highly specialized roles, prevent smooth transfer of duties and organizational/process knowledge; mitigants: appropriate workforce planning, documentation of duties and performed tasks enabling reconstruction by others, and hiring new employees. Labour-law abuse, mobbing or discrimination, and occupational health and safety violations may cause loss of key competences and human capital, compensation-proceeding costs, significant reputational risk weakening organizational culture, lower client-service quality, and operational errors from destabilized internal processes; controls: anonymous surveys and analysis, whistleblowing system, work regulations, support from local law firms, training, webinars, implementation of the Helping Hand wellbeing platform providing psychological support to employees, and cooperation with a mediator.
