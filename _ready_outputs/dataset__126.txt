XTB S.A. Group 2025 consolidated annual report excerpt describes XTB as an international broker with geographically diversified revenue; xStation and the XTB mobile application serve clients in Central and Eastern Europe, Western Europe, the Middle East and Latin America. Strategic priority: foreign expansion and access to mass clients worldwide, pursued continuously through organic creation of subsidiaries and potential mergers or acquisitions, especially entities offering geographic synergies and only where transactions provide measurable benefits for the Company and shareholders. Group revenue generally benefits from higher financial and commodity market activity, because such periods bring higher client trading volumes and higher profitability per lot; clear, long market trends are most favorable and typically support the highest revenue. Lower market activity and fewer client transactions reduce operating revenue and profitability. More predictable range-bound markets support range trading, increasing profitable client transactions and reducing Group result or generating market-making losses. Market volatility and activity depend on external market-specific and macroeconomic factors and may materially affect quarterly revenue, a feature of the Group business model. In 2025 XTB developed operations in Indonesia, acquired first clients, ran intensive marketing to build brand awareness, and its Indonesian subsidiary offered shares and ETFs, with CFDs planned for addition at the turn of 2025/2026. XTB completed the formal process in Brazil, obtained an operating permit and entered the supervised institutions list, but due to current conditions in the Brazilian brokerage sector, particularly local protectionism, expects to discontinue further activity on that market. The Group expanded its licence in the United Arab Emirates, enabling stronger positioning in the fast-growing Middle East region. Europe remained one of XTB main markets; the Management Board regularly monitors branches and subsidiaries to identify opportunities to increase XTB share in each geography. Client assets at 31 December 2025 totaled 45,802M PLN versus 27,460M PLN at 31 December 2024. Client cash exceeded 5.8bn PLN and amounted to 5,864M PLN versus 3,751M PLN, up 56.3% year on year. Nominal value of instruments deposited by clients on brokerage accounts rose 99.4% year on year to 27,284M PLN versus 13,682M PLN. Nominal value of client derivative transactions increased by 2.6bn PLN to 12,654M PLN versus 10,027M PLN. XTB intended in 2025 to acquire an average of at least 150-210 thousand new clients per quarter; the 2025 versus 2024 quarterly new-client chart includes 2025 figures of 280,881 in 1Q, 194,304 in 2Q, 167,339 in 3Q and 221,762 in 4Q, compared with 129,747, 102,569, 108,104 and 158,018 in the corresponding 2024 quarters. For 2026 the Management Board ambition is to acquire on average at least 250-290 thousand new clients quarterly; in January and February of the current year XTB acquired 246,438 new clients. In the medium term, defined as the three-year 2027-2029 horizon, the Management Board will seek roughly 30% year-on-year growth in new clients, assuming average client acquisition cost remains at a level similar to 2023-2026. The product mix chart for 2025 shows 20% CFDs, 14% Investment Plans, 27% ETFs and 39% shares, compared with 21%, 17%, 26% and 36% respectively in 2024, indicating a lower CFD and Investment Plan share and higher ETF and share share. By declared country of residence, most investors came from Europe, Latin America and Asia; Europe accounted for 88.8% of clients in 2025 versus 87.3% in 2024. The average age of a retail investor, based on analysis of all open, closed and maintained positions in 2025, was 35 years 3 months versus 35 years 1 month a year earlier. The largest client age segment remained 25-44 years, while the 25-34 and 35-44 groups reduced their shares by 1.1 and 0.7 percentage points respectively. The youngest 18-24 group increased its share by 0.3 percentage point and the 45-54 group by 1.4 percentage points; oldest investor groups aged 55 and above remained unchanged. The 2025 age-structure chart shows 18-24 at 14.0%, 25-34 at 35.2%, 35-44 at 26.7%, 45-54 at 18.5%, 55-64 at 4.1%, 65-74 at 1.1% and 75 or older at 0.2%, versus 13.7%, 36.3%, 27.4%, 17.1%, 4.2%, 1.1% and 0.2% in 2024.
