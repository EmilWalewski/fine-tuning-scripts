XTB Group 2025 report excerpt covers macro, market and geopolitical environment. In the euro area, the ECB adjusted policy to gradual disinflation and moderate growth; in H1 2025 it continued easing, cutting March 2025 rates to a 2.50% deposit rate, 2.65% refinancing rate and 2.90% marginal lending rate in response to slower growth and disinflation progress. Later the Governing Council kept rates unchanged; from September 2025 the deposit rate stayed at 2.00% and the refinancing rate at 2.15%, as inflation was close to the medium-term target of about 2% and current rates were considered adequate. European equity markets in 2025 combined volatility, strong share-price gains and moderate investor optimism; the STOXX Europe 600 reached record levels in February 2026, exceeded 630 points in one session, and was led especially by financial, raw-material, banking and industrial companies. IPO activity partly recovered: in Q3 2025 Europe completed more than a dozen public offerings worth over EUR 2.3bn. European equities were mixed, with optimism waves, stronger indices, volatility episodes and reactions to global factors; improving company results and macro stabilization drew capital to blue chips and higher-growth banks, defence and industry. Poland’s WIG index rose about 47% y/y and set all-time highs in 2025; gains covered large, mid-cap and smaller companies, showing improved sentiment across equities. Turnover also increased: 2025 GPW main-market equity trading reached about PLN 470.3bn, up about 42% y/y, and average session equity trading was PLN 1.89bn, also about 42% above 2024. December 2025 was the record month, with total equity turnover of PLN 44.7bn, up 76.2% y/y, including order-book turnover of PLN 38.2bn, up 53.2% y/y. Higher liquidity reflected greater foreign investor involvement and domestic investor interest, making 2025 one of the strongest years for the Polish equity market in the last decade. The EU and euro-area economy grew moderately in 2025: the European Commission spring forecast indicated euro-area real GDP growth of about 0.9% and EU growth of about 1.1%, continuing moderate expansion. Euro-area inflation gradually declined and hovered near the ECB target of about 2%; expert projections put headline inflation at about 2.1% in 2025, with further fading price pressure expected and weaker energy and consumer-goods cost dynamics visible. Globally, IMF data and OECD forecasts showed 2025 growth at 3.1%-3.4%, average global GDP about 3.2% and OECD estimated real global growth at 3.1%, slower than pre-pandemic years; 2026 growth was expected at about 3.0%-3.1% because of trade restrictions, geopolitical tensions and weaker investment. Global CPI inflation declined but stayed relevant for monetary policy, stabilising around 3.6% in 2025, with developed economies often near central-bank targets and emerging markets somewhat above. US GDP grew about 2.2% in 2025, slower than earlier high-growth years; CPI fell to about 2.7% at year-end, approaching the Federal Reserve 2% target, while core inflation stabilized, allowing neutral rates and cautious monetary policy. China recorded about 4.5% GDP growth in 2025, among the strongest major-economy results despite weaker real estate and investment; National Bureau of Statistics data showed CPI down 0.1% y/y in May 2025 and down 0.3% in September 2025, confirming near-zero or deflationary consumer-price conditions, so People’s Bank of China policy focused more on supporting growth than limiting inflation. Geopolitical risks included Russia’s invasion of Ukraine on 24 February 2022, when Russian troops crossed Ukraine’s eastern, southern and northern borders; the EU and many other countries imposed severe sanctions on Russia, mainly restricting strategic sectors’ access to technologies and markets. The war had no material direct impact on the Group, but caused high volatility in global financial and commodity markets, affecting XTB client transaction activity and Group results. In early March 2026, Middle East escalation included Iranian attacks on infrastructure in Dubai, United Arab Emirates, disrupting transport of about 20% of global oil exports; oil rose 6%-10% short term, increasing commodity and financial-market volatility and energy costs. XTB has two Dubai subsidiaries, monitors them continuously and sees no material negative impact from the conflict on regional operations. In 2025 Donald Trump’s administration introduced significant customs tariffs affecting global trade and financial markets; the average effective US tariff rate rose from about 2.3% at the beginning of 2025 to about 9.8%-15% by year-end.
