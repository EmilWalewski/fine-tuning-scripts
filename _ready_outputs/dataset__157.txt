XTB risk disclosure describes client and company exposure to foreign-exchange effects from instruments held directly and from mismatches between portfolio instruments and the settlement currency in which client deposits are held. Interest-rate and yield-curve risk is material for XTB because rate changes can affect net interest income: the company holds cash on interest-bearing bank accounts, and changes in interest rates also affect the interest terms and valuation of debt instruments, including government and corporate bonds, potentially held within liquid-asset management. For clients, interest-rate risk appears through changes in position-maintenance costs, swaps, financing costs and valuation of rate-sensitive instruments. Market-risk management procedures include client position limits and instrument position limits. Investment policy covers investment categories, allocation limits, maximum maturity, maximum exposure to issuers of financial instruments and minimum required credit ratings of banks. XTB also identifies risk of results dependent on market conditions, including low activity, low volatility and lack of trends. Because the business model materially involves CFD transactions with clients under a market-maker formula, market volatility is important for achieving expected financial results; prolonged low volatility may reduce client interest in CFDs, with lower CFD market turnover translating into weaker company results. Mitigation includes revenue-source diversification, including commissions, shares and ETFs, cost management, offer development and geographic market development. Trading-system failures may require corrections in the trading system in favor of clients and reduce XTB's financial result; publication of significant trading-system outages in the media may damage reputation, drive existing clients away and discourage potential clients from using XTB services. Technical trading-system error risk covers unexpected and inappropriate system behavior, including an erroneous hedging order placed on the market when hedging is unnecessary or should have different parameters, creating excessive risk exposure, possible breach of regulatory capital-adequacy standards and additional costs. Another error type is execution at an incorrect price, requiring adjustment of the client account balance; in some cases, system errors may credit undue gains to a client account and the client may withdraw them before detection. Trading-system errors may also lead to accounting-book and report errors. Controls include root-cause analysis, trading-system optimization, price reconciliation and validation of price sources. Incorrect data in the trading system may cause adverse client outcomes, including losses from transactions at erroneous prices, position closure through an unnecessarily triggered stop-out mechanism or erroneous client investment decisions. Such events may generate client complaints, corrections to client account balances and a reduction in XTB's result. Mitigation includes validation of price sources by comparison with a backup source, verification of quotation correctness when materially different from the previous quotation, monitoring and limitation of quotation spread size, mechanisms ensuring execution and price continuity, quotation controls by the Trading Department and Control and Risk Department, and trading-system optimization. Incorrect operation of the algorithmic trading algorithm may produce client-account financial-instrument balances inconsistent with expectations, potentially requiring recognition of client claims; controls include self-assessment and validation of the algorithmic trading system, monitoring results achieved by client portfolios and periodic verification of algorithm correctness. Failure of the market-risk management system xRisk or the snapshot module materially limits effective risk management because absent appropriate xRisk information the company cannot determine current risk exposure, hedge-position values or instruments on which hedges should be opened. Consequences include possible failure to keep capital requirements at a level consistent with risk appetite and risk of breaching capital-adequacy standards. Mitigation includes an xRisk backup, continuous xRisk development, optimization and bug fixing, xRisk monitoring, and, if the snapshot module alone fails, the ability to estimate risk using a backup tool prepared by the Risk Control Department based on current positions in xRisk. Hardware or network failure in the server room may paralyze the trading platform, including xStation, cutting clients off from order execution and liquidity, generating direct revenue losses and affecting regulatory compliance. For clients, this means loss of control over open positions, especially in high market volatility, causing mass complaints, disputes and a sharp fall in trust in service stability. Broader market consequences include exposure to supervisory sanctions for lack of operational resilience under DORA and risk of permanent user outflow to competitors. Controls include tripling computer hardware in the server room with parallel operation, real-time monitoring, and BCM and DR plans and tests. Loss of the server room due to failure, including fire, flooding or sabotage, is described as a catastrophic event for XTB that immediately paralyzes the value chain. For active clients it means inability to manage investment exposure, potentially generating mass financial losses and legal claims during volatility. Strategically, lack of immediate switchover to backup infrastructure exposes the firm to severe regulator sanctions, including KNF and FCA, for breach of business-continuity requirements and damages the brand reputation whose competitive advantage is based on stability and speed. Mitigation includes selection of a certified server room, server-room diversification, qualitative assessment of service providers, business-continuity procedures, assessment of exposure to natural disasters within ESG risk management and supplier audits. Computer or equipment failures affecting key employees, including Trading, IT or Customer Support, create local but material operational bottlenecks, preventing effective market-risk management and ongoing service of active-client inquiries; during high market volatility they may reduce client-service quality, causing image losses and client loss. Controls include systematic review of equipment condition and repair or replacement, plus IT monitoring and analysis of workstation condition.
