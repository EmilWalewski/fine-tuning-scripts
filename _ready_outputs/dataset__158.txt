XTB S.A. Group presents fair value measurement hierarchy for financial instruments measured at fair value: Level 1 quoted unadjusted active-market prices, Level 2 observable direct or indirect inputs other than Level 1 prices including less active quoted prices and other valuation techniques using market-derived inputs, Level 3 unobservable inputs. At 31.12.2025, financial assets measured in the hierarchy totalled 1,006,973, comprising 248,525 in Level 1 and 758,448 in Level 2, with no Level 3 balance; financial liabilities measured at fair value through profit or loss totalled 271,159, entirely Level 2. At 31.12.2024, financial assets totalled 1,123,923, comprising 592,116 in Level 1 and 531,807 in Level 2, with no Level 3 balance; financial liabilities measured at fair value through profit or loss totalled 208,193, entirely Level 2. Year on year, fair-value financial assets decreased by 116,950, Level 1 assets decreased by 343,591, Level 2 assets increased by 226,641, and fair-value financial liabilities increased by 62,966. No transfers occurred between fair-value hierarchy levels in the periods covered by the consolidated financial statements. Fair value of contracts for differences (CFDs) is determined from market prices of underlying instruments obtained from independent sources, including verified liquidity providers and reputable information services, adjusted by the Group’s spread; valuation uses closing prices or last bid and ask prices. CFDs are measured as the difference between current price and opening price, including accrued commissions and swap points. The Group assessed counterparty credit-risk valuation adjustments as immaterial to the overall valuation of derivative transactions and, under IFRS 13.73, does not classify those derivative transactions in Level 3 because unobservable inputs are not significant. During the reporting period, the Group traded OTC CFDs and acquired securities; it may also enter futures contracts for its own account on regulated exchanges. Market-risk categories comprise foreign exchange risk, interest-rate risk, commodity price risk and equity-instrument price risk. The primary objective of market-risk management is to limit negative effects on operating profitability. Internal procedures impose limits on market risk from open financial-instrument positions, including maximum open position per instrument, currency exposure limits and single-order value limits. The Trading Department monitors open positions against limits on an ongoing basis and executes hedging transactions when limits are breached; the Risk Control Department regularly checks limit utilisation and the rationale for any hedging transactions. The Group mainly enters transactions in instruments exposed to FX risk; beyond instruments whose underlying is an exchange rate, it offers instruments priced in foreign currencies and holds foreign-currency assets, including brokerage-house own funds denominated in foreign currencies for settlement on foreign markets and for foreign branches. Changes in exchange rates, particularly PLN versus foreign currencies, affect balance-sheet valuation of financial instruments and translation results on foreign-currency balances for other balance-sheet items. FX sensitivity assumes a parallel +/-5% change of all foreign currencies against PLN and remeasurement of financial instruments. For the 12 months ended 31.12.2025, a 5% exchange-rate increase would raise profit before tax by PLN 75.4M and equity by PLN 6.8M, while a 5% decrease would reduce profit before tax by PLN 75.4M and equity by PLN 6.8M. For the 12 months ended 31.12.2024, the corresponding impacts were PLN 73.6M on profit before tax and PLN 4.9M on equity in either direction. Equity sensitivity relates to exchange differences from translating foreign operations whose equity is held in functional currencies. Interest-rate risk is the risk that current and future financial result and capital are adversely affected by interest-rate changes. Sources include contracts creating interest-rate-dependent receivables or liabilities, interest-rate-linked financial assets or liabilities, volatility in interest on bank accounts and deposits holding the Group’s own cash, mismatch between interest rates paid to clients on free cash held in their cash accounts, and interest-rate effects on valuation of Treasury bonds, bonds guaranteed by the State Treasury and corporate bonds held by the Group. Additional profit or loss volatility linked to market interest rates arises from amounts paid and received because of interest-rate differentials between currencies, i.e. swap points, and from holding debt financial instruments. Because the Group maintains low duration of assets and liabilities and minimises the duration gap, sensitivity of market value of assets and liabilities to market interest-rate changes is low.
