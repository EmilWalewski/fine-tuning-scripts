XTB S.A. Capital Group Consolidated Annual Report 2025 — risk management section. The Group's principal risk exposures comprise market, credit, liquidity and operational risk (the latter encompassing compliance/legal risk and cyber-threat risk). Risk Control Department regularly verifies limit utilization and the justification for any hedging transactions, maintaining risks at acceptable levels.

Market risk arises directly from the business model of offering CFD derivatives (currencies, indices, commodities) under a market-maker model, whereby XTB is counterparty to client-initiated transactions. Financial-market volatility is the key determinant of instrument results; Company performance is tied to client transactional activity and macroeconomic parameters (interest rates, inflation, FX rates), strongly affecting short-term profitability and operational burden. Internal procedures apply limits restricting market risk on open positions: maximum open-position limit per financial instrument, single-order value limit, and per-client exposure limit. The Trading Department continuously monitors limited open positions and, on breaches, executes hedging transactions that can be undertaken rapidly to immediately reduce market-risk exposure.

Credit risk concentrates primarily on counterparty risk from holding significant cash at banking institutions and cooperation with liquidity providers. Retail-client credit risk is marginal: the business model relies on prepayment mechanisms and automatic stop-out systems closing positions before negative balances arise. The principal threat is potential insolvency of financial institutions holding own and client funds. Management entails rigorous diversification of placed funds, selection of partners with high investment ratings (e.g., Fitch, S&P), and ongoing monitoring of counterparty financial condition to keep concentration risk within acceptable limits.

Liquidity risk is assessed as relatively low, as the value of the most liquid assets (own cash) substantially exceeds the Group's current liabilities. Material exposure sources nonetheless include maturity mismatch between asset maturities and liability obligations, and potential sharp increases in client cash withdrawals during heightened market volatility, plus the need to maintain adequate liquidity buffers on accounts enabling hedging transactions. The Group maintains procedures including emergency liquidity plans; owing to its strong liquidity position, no plan measures required activation. Liquidity-risk management rests on continuous monitoring of current/future financial needs against available cash. The key mitigating mechanism is avoiding excessive capital allocation to limited-liquidity assets (long-term deposits, bonds) that could generate losses on sudden liquidation. Policy assumes maintaining a safe level of free funds for operational continuity regardless of market conditions, supported by rigorous bank-partner selection and cash-level monitoring versus liquid-asset demand.

Operational risk: given dynamic development of the parent entity, product-offer expansion and IT-infrastructure buildout, the Company is largely exposed to operational risk (loss from inadequate/failed internal processes, human and system errors, or external events). Legal risk and compliance risk are treated as types of operational risk. Procedures include business-continuity plans, contingency plans and HR policy.

Sensitivity analysis (PLN exchange-rate movements vs foreign currencies, impact on equity and pre-tax profit/loss), period 12 months ended: A 5% increase in exchange rates affects pre-tax profit/(loss) by +69.1M PLN (FY2025) / +70.2M PLN (FY2024), and a 5% decrease by (69.1M) PLN / (70.2M) PLN respectively. Equity impact: +1.7M PLN (FY2025) / +1.3M PLN (FY2024) on a 5% rate increase, and (1.7M) PLN / (1.3M) PLN on a 5% decrease — indicating a slight year-over-year decline in pre-tax profit sensitivity and a modest rise in equity sensitivity (figures in thousands PLN, converted to millions).

Cybersecurity risk: escalating digitalization, the Company's strongly technological DNA and rising cyber-threats (account/asset access by attackers, unauthorized transactions and transfers, confidential-data theft, DDoS attacks) drive loss and reputational risks. Mitigation: internal regulations and processes increasing resilience — cyclical employee security-awareness training, employee/client data-leak detection, a vulnerability-management and incident-response system, market-participant collaboration for threat-information exchange, two-factor authentication (2FA), additional authorization for material account changes, anomalous-transaction monitoring for intrusion indicators, and security-culture campaigns.

ESG risk: approach evolved from image issue to integral risk-management architecture element, formalized via the new Sustainable Development Strategy 2024–2027 and a comprehensive Double Materiality Assessment. Identified exposures: environmental physical and transition risks (sharp energy-price increases, server-room continuity threats from natural disasters); social/governance risks centered on human-capital management (including diversity policy) and data security/cybersecurity as foundations of client trust. The ESG Team, led by the Chief Financial Officer (Management Board Member for Finance), handles climate-risk identification, verification and monitoring; the Risk Control Department embeds ESG risks into XTB's internal Risk Management System, translating non-financial risks into ICAAP capital-adequacy terminology and integrating them into internal reporting/control. Management uses hard data and procedures, including energy-price stress testing and ESG criteria in IT-infrastructure supplier assessment.
