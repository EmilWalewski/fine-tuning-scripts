XTB Group reports that Russia’s 24 February 2022 invasion of Ukraine and related sanctions do not currently have a material impact on the Group, although they previously increased financial and commodity market volatility, affected client trading activity, and influenced 2022 results. The Group also notes a March 2026 escalation in the Middle East, when Iran attacked infrastructure in Dubai, disrupting transport linked to around 20% of global oil exports and briefly lifting oil prices by 6-10%, which increased commodity and financial market volatility and energy costs; despite having two subsidiaries in Dubai, XTB currently sees no material negative impact on regional operations and continues monitoring the situation. Accounting policies used in the 2025 consolidated financial statements are consistent with those applied for the year ended 31 December 2024, except for new or amended standards effective from 1 January 2025, including IAS 21 amendments on lack of exchangeability, which did not materially affect the consolidated statements. The Group did not early adopt standards not yet effective under EU rules and does not expect upcoming IFRS 9, IFRS 7, IFRS 18, or IFRS 19 changes to materially affect its consolidated financial statements. Management applies professional judgment and estimates in determining asset and liability carrying amounts, with assumptions continuously reviewed against historical experience and expected future events. Under IFRS 9, XTB recognizes expected credit loss allowances for amortized-cost assets using forecasts and expected economic conditions, records impairments when post-recognition evidence indicates reduced expected cash flows, and assesses overdue receivables for threatened or doubtful recovery. Fixed assets, including intangibles, are reviewed annually for impairment indicators such as licence expiry or withdrawal from use, with recoverable amounts estimated when needed and carrying values reduced accordingly. Deferred tax assets are recognized only when future taxable income is probable, mainly for tax losses of foreign branches in early development, assessed under local tax rules and future tax budgets.
