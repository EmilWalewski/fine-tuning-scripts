Excerpt from Management Board Report on operations of Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group for 2025 (Consolidated Sustainability Reporting, section SBM-2: Interests and Views of Stakeholders). Documents the Group's stakeholder identification and engagement framework underpinning its double-materiality assessment. The key stakeholder list was derived from a management-led stakeholder assessment evaluating each group's strength of influence, based on managerial expertise, professional experience, and the frequency and significance of contacts relative to execution of business strategy, timeliness, quality, and effectiveness of actions. Identified key stakeholders: local communities, public authorities, business partners/contractors, and capital investors; despite lower assessment scores, property managers, workers in the value chain, and end users were also retained on the final key-stakeholder list given their significance across the value chain.

Per-stakeholder profiles, classifications, communication channels, and engagement measures: Own workforce (internal) — engaged via surveys, in-depth interviews, ESG-focused surveys, training, newsletters; regarded as a key resource influencing firm value and productivity, seeking stable employment, professional development, and association with an ethically governed firm of strong market position. Engagement examples: internal-communication initiatives (intranet expansion, internal newsletter), employee-integration activities (integration trips, joint charitable actions), and competence development for large, complex project delivery and a professional work environment.

Workers in the value chain (external) — primarily subcontractor employees working on construction sites; communication via training and newsletters. Characterized by high cultural diversity and frequent rotation inherent to the work, materially affecting timeliness and quality of investments. Expectations vary by role/responsibility and local market conditions; site workers seek safe, stable working conditions, appropriate protective equipment, transparent communication, and mutual respect. Engagement: raising occupational health and safety (BHP/OHS) standards on sites, implementing comprehensive OHS programs, process optimization to support communication and ongoing cooperation, and requirements on subcontractor employment conditions; ensuring safe working conditions and fair employment principles is deemed material.

Local communities (external, surveys) — residents of estates surrounding investments, urban movements, associations, neighborhood councils, and social-good/urban-quality organizations. Group companies are assessed by communities for impact on urban fabric and residents' quality of life, valuing aesthetics of urban space, development of missing infrastructure, and local social engagement. Engagement: financial support for local sporting and cultural events, multi-stage projects supplementing missing infrastructure in major agglomerations, integration of sustainability into investment planning and execution (including publicly accessible green spaces), and delivery/support of local pro-social projects.

Competition (sector peers, external, ESG reports) — indirectly drives organizational development through market rivalry, expecting fair competition, efficiency gains, and innovation in project delivery; the competitive environment compels construction firms toward differentiation while maintaining good industry relations. Engagement: raising external sustainability-related communication, raising industry standards (material quality, deployed innovations and on-estate solutions, additional customer amenities), market-awareness building and improved industry PR, participation in industry initiatives, digitalization solutions (BIM, electronic signatures), and promotion of business transparency and ethics via report publication. Non-financial report users are also noted.

Public authorities (external; surveys and ESG-focused surveys) — including public-private partnership (PPP) context; as lawmakers they exercise a control function over organizational actions, safeguard the public interest and free-market principles, and assess the business sector's progress toward public objectives. Expectations relate to implementation of local policies, regulations, and guidelines; Group companies play an important role in residential and infrastructure investments. Engagement: aligning processes, procedures, and policies with national regulatory requirements; value creation and risk reduction through compliance development; timely investment delivery; ensuring transparency; and development of public infrastructure.

Business partners/contractors (external, surveys) — without robust, efficiently functioning supply chains no organization can properly meet its goals; contractors require information on the firm's sustainability performance relevant to their own sustainability objectives and cooperation decisions, expecting reliability, timeliness, and long-term cooperation in supply of materials and services. Engagement: improved communication, more effective risk management via faster reactions to supply-chain events, transparent cooperation principles building long-term business relationships, and maintenance of large-scale operations ensuring regular orders, predictability, and stable cooperation.

Property managers (external; surveys and ESG-focused surveys) — tasked with maintaining properties in good condition; cooperate with Group companies mainly on property modernization and maintenance. Engagement: providing feedback on applied design solutions. No financial figures, monetary amounts, or dates beyond the 2025 reporting year are present in this excerpt.
