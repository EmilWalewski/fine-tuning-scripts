Excerpt from the consolidated sustainability statement and Management Board report on operations of Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group for FY2025 (Grupa Kapitałowa Dom Development S.A.), covering ESRS SBM-3 material impacts, risks and opportunities and their interrelation with strategy and the business model. Own workforce (defined as persons on employment contracts) is identified as a key element of strategy and the business model; employee development and well-being yield synergies between organizational goals and employee expectations, with workforce creating added value, delivering it to clients and building durable competitive advantage. As of report publication date, the Group does not perceive current nor anticipate future effects of material risks and opportunities on its business model, value chain, strategy or decision-making. No widespread or systemic negative human-rights impacts occur across Group companies, for own-workforce employees or value-chain workers. During revision of the double materiality process, a negative impact regarding the sector's exposure to forced labour was identified. No material impacts on own workforce arising from transition plans (to reduce environmental impacts and increase ecological/climate-neutral character of operations) were identified. Value-chain workforce analysis focused on workers of entities contracting with general-contractor companies for defined construction scopes matching their profile, specialization and capacity; these persons work at Group investment sites but are not own employees. Group associates comprise persons on civil-law contracts and B2B contracts. The Group identified entity-specific disclosures tied to operations: managing relations with surroundings and co-creating urban fabric, with IROs relating to local communities (residents of estates surrounding investments, urban movements, associations, estate councils, social-good organizations). ESRS S4 disclosure scope covers all Group clients and end-users, groups particularly exposed to marketing strategies regarding information on offered investments.

Climate-related IRO tables (short/medium/long-term horizons). Long-term: risk of very high post-completion building adaptation costs; potential asset impairment and lower market attractiveness; long-term energy cost increases may affect profitability; decarbonization may become competitive advantage, boosting long-term profitability; use of renewable energy sources. Short/medium term: rising operating costs from more frequent repairs, design changes and schedule delays; possible shifts in client purchasing patterns toward low-emission buildings; decarbonization spending may raise operating costs; investments in emission-reducing technologies (renewables) may initially reduce cash flows due to high implementation costs but improve profitability via redemption targets; energy price rises burden production and material costs.

Risks/opportunities: halting/delay of construction works and supply-chain disruption from sudden weather events (intense rainfall, strong winds); high post-completion adaptation costs versus designing investments with extra weather safeguards. Transition risk: changing purchasing patterns from higher electricity/heat costs and regulatory compliance; increased decarbonization outlays to meet reduction targets; building carbon-footprint reduction. Topic table (Climate change): buildings currently not formally assessed for climate-change adaptation solutions that could reduce identified physical risks; buildings meet applicable technical-condition requirements on energy performance, air-tightness, acoustics and integrity but are not aligned to the EPBD Directive. Climate change mitigation / GHG emissions: Group companies aim to cut Scope 1 and 2 emissions by gradually increasing contracting of renewable-source energy; construction sector's high emissivity tied to purchase/processing/recycling of building materials, where lower-emission materials may cut long-term costs but require significant financial outlays. Greater energy spending reduces funds for other investments. Transition risk: energy price growth from Poland's high-emission energy mix raising operating costs; land-availability constraints from potential exclusion of floodplain and agricultural land, affecting long-term cash-flow growth; potential asset impairment in areas of rising climate threat; higher operating costs from stricter regulations. Engagement in innovations such as Power Purchase Agreements (PPA) for long-term renewable use. Physical risk: flooding and infrastructure damage; rising insurance costs; asset-value loss; offset by creating new publicly accessible green areas improving company image. Energy: growing energy demand for construction works. Biodiversity and ecosystems: Group affects biodiversity via own activity causing land-use change (and freshwater/marine use change); investments located on floodplains may increase climate-change vulnerability; direct resource exploitation (felling trees and shrubs); environmental decision and compensation costs; rising environmental-use fees; offsetting opportunities include strategic value growth (greater real-estate portfolio resilience), operational stability from lower sanction/delay/remediation risk, and long-term savings from more efficient resource management improving cash flows. Steadily rising digital-security maintenance costs may affect profitability.
