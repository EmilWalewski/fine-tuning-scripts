XTB S.A. Capital Group Consolidated Annual Report 2025, ESG/workforce ("own workforce") disclosure section. To improve internal communication XTB introduced bilingual messaging, an intranet, the xPress employee newsletter, "Town Hall" meetings and internal mailing; employees have direct contact with supervisors and Management Board members, and the CEO communicates financial results and strategic plans during recurring online meetings, supplemented by integration events. In 2025 XTB executed its 2024-2027 ESG Strategy, updated in 2024 based on a stakeholder-opinion survey conducted as part of a double-materiality assessment; employee representatives participated in workshops and in-depth interviews, identifying material impacts, risks and opportunities (described under ESRS 2 SBM-3, chapter 1.6 Statements). Strategic objectives were defined with staff responsible for the HR area, who report progress to the Sustainability Team. Positive impacts identified include employment flexibility, cooperation with employee representatives and implemented workforce policies/regulations; negative impacts (actual and potential) include pay differences (gender pay gap) and insufficiently developed communication not keeping pace with the Group's dynamic growth. The pay gap, calculated for the XTB Group for the first time in 2024, drove reinforcement of internal communication (xPress newsletter, regular Town Hall meetings) and strengthening of the remuneration area, including establishment of a dedicated remuneration organizational unit.

Average training hours per employee: 2025 figure (no gender/grade split) 15.14 hours vs 7.25 in 2024, a +108.8% YoY increase. By grade (2025 vs 2024 with YoY): Senior management and directors 21.00 vs 10.38 (+102.3%); Middle/managerial staff 19.61 vs 9.54 (+105.6%); Other employees 13.86 vs 6.72 (+106.1%). By gender, male training hours rose most (+130.9%) versus +70.9% for women, linked to male predominance in employment (67% of employees) and their performance of roles requiring mandatory specialist training; training-hour growth dynamics significantly exceeded headcount growth for both genders. The overall increase reflects improved monitoring of exact training time and a higher number of trainings versus 2024.

Planned employee appraisals (number, by grade, gender split female): Senior management and directors 40; Middle/managerial staff 183; Other employees 1,084; Total 1,307. Completed employee appraisals: 28; 113; 546; total 687.

Key HR policies disclosed: HR Policy supporting effective human-resource management via headcount planning, recruitment, development support and team retention; Remuneration Policy defining pay terms, work-related benefits and associated employer/employee rights and obligations; Anti-mobbing and Anti-discrimination Policy responding to the ESG Strategy and building an ethical organizational culture; and the Policy for Managing Diversity, Equal Treatment and Inclusion Culture (Diversity/DEI Policy, S1-1 MDR-P), created in response to identified material impacts/risks/opportunities, implemented by the Talent department and adopted by Management Board resolution, governing diversity and inclusion across pay, education and recruitment with a violation-reporting mechanism, communicated to and known by employees. Per MDR-T, despite no defined targets/effectiveness metrics for the Diversity Policy, its effectiveness is monitored through identified violation cases.
