Excerpt from XTB S.A. Capital Group consolidated annual report 2025 (consolidated financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2025), sections 4.4.1-4.6, setting out financial-instrument and fixed-asset accounting policies. Financial assets are measured at amortized cost where both conditions hold: the asset is held under a business model whose objective is collecting contractual cash flows, and contractual terms give rise on specified dates to cash flows that are solely payments of principal and interest on outstanding principal; interest income is computed using the effective interest rate method and reported within "Finance income" in the statement of comprehensive income. Financial assets failing the amortized-cost criteria are measured at fair value through profit or loss (FVTPL); gains/losses on remeasurement of debt investments to fair value are recognized in profit or loss; dividends are recognized when the entity's right to receive payment is established; the Group classifies mainly OTC derivatives and listed equities into this category. Fair value is the price receivable on sale of an asset or payable on transfer of a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date; for instruments on an active market fair value is measured using quoted market prices (an active market exists where quoted prices are readily and regularly available and represent actual, current arm's-length transactions); absent an active market, valuation models are applied; initial fair value equals the transaction price. The Group applies valuation techniques appropriate to circumstances, maximizing observable and minimizing unobservable inputs, using a three-level fair-value hierarchy: Level 1 - fully observable inputs (active-market quotations); Level 2 - models using inputs other than Level 1 but observable directly/indirectly (quotations for similar assets/liabilities from active or inactive markets); Level 3 - models using unobservable inputs (not from an active market); techniques are applied consistently. Impairment: the Group recognizes an expected credit loss (ECL) allowance on financial assets measured at amortized cost, assessing objective evidence of impairment at each reporting period-end; ECL is probability-weighted credit loss measured at either 12-month ECL or lifetime ECL where a significant increase in credit risk since recognition or impairment is identified; assets are staged at each reporting date; ECL is calculated on initial recognition of a receivable and updated each subsequent reporting date depending on days past due, with the initial expected loss and each subsequent increase recognized in profit or loss. Financial liabilities at amortized cost (including bank loans and borrowings) are initially measured at fair value less transaction costs, then at amortized cost using the effective interest rate method. Financial liabilities at FVTPL comprise held-for-trading liabilities (undertaken primarily for short-term repurchase; forming part of a managed portfolio of instruments managed together under an actual pattern of short-term profit-taking; or derivatives not designated/effective as hedges); an entity may, on initial recognition, irrevocably designate a financial liability as FVTPL where this yields more relevant information; such liabilities are carried at fair value with resulting finance gains/losses recognized in period income or costs, including interest paid on the liability. Intangible assets (section 4.5) are non-physical, identifiable, reliably measurable assets expected to generate future economic benefits; initially recognized at purchase price or production cost, then measured at cost less accumulated amortization and any impairment; the Group holds no indefinite-life intangibles and uses straight-line amortization over useful lives: software licenses 5 years, internally generated intangibles 5 years, other intangibles 10 years. Property, plant and equipment (section 4.6) covers PP&E and outlays on PP&E under construction intended for operational/administrative use beyond 1 year, including investment outlays and prepayments for future deliveries of machinery, equipment and services (advances paid); initially recognized at purchase price or production cost, with significant components recognized separately, then measured at cost less accumulated depreciation and any permanent impairment; depreciation begins the month after use commences, useful-life estimates are reviewed annually, and straight-line depreciation is applied over useful lives: mobile phones 2 years, computers 3 to 5 years, transport vehicles 5 years, office furniture and equipment 5 to 12 years.
