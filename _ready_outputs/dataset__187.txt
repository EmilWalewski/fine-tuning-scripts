Macroeconomic environment: XTB Group operations are affected by macroeconomic conditions and global market volatility; inflation, interest rates, FX rates, and international events shape client behavior, investment appetite, and product preferences. High inflation erodes real purchasing power, driving investors toward capital-protection and long-term-savings instruments across asset classes including exchange-listed shares, ETFs, CFDs, corporate and government bonds, structured products, and real estate or commodity funds. Interest-rate changes affect financing costs, deposit/bond attractiveness, and expected returns, influencing capital allocation and medium/long-term savings strategies. Global political and economic events (e.g., US trade-policy shifts, armed conflicts, geopolitical tensions) generate market volatility, affect FX and asset prices, and drive client investment activity and XTB financial results.

Poland macroeconomic environment 2025: economy operated amid stabilizing conditions and gradual easing of inflationary pressure. Per preliminary GUS data, real GDP growth for full-year 2025 reached 3.6% YoY, a clear acceleration versus 3.0% in 2024, confirming stronger economic activity across the year, driven primarily by private consumption and recovering investment activity. Inflation declined systematically through the year; GUS preliminary CPI reading was 2.4% YoY in November 2025, a material slowdown versus prior months. Monetary policy was cautious: in the first half NBP reference rate held at 5.75%, reflecting prior monetary tightening; as inflation prospects improved, the Monetary Policy Council (RPP) began easing in the second half, cutting rates to approximately 4.25%. Per European Commission forecasts, Poland's 2026 real GDP growth is projected at approximately 3.5% on stable private consumption and increased EU-fund-related investment; CPI inflation is expected to continue fading to around 2.9% on an annual-average basis, keeping inflation near the NBP target with moderately low price-growth pace.

Capital market: 2025 was a period of very strong market conditions and clear valuation gains among companies listed on the Warsaw Stock Exchange (GPW). The WIG20 index of largest companies ended the year up approximately 45% YoY, exceeding the 3,000-point level in August and reaching its highest values for the first time since May 2008, reflecting strong equity-market dynamics on GPW and improved investor sentiment after years of consolidation. Gains were particularly pronounced in the financial sector and among the largest commodity and energy companies, which materially contributed to the index result.
