Excerpt from the Consolidated Annual Report of the XTB S.A. Capital Group 2025, detailing the Group's risk-factor taxonomy, mitigation measures, and ESG framework. The text enumerates qualitative risk categories with associated drivers, consequences for client/market/firm, and mitigants.

Technology-competence shortfall: market-scale deficit in technological skills may weaken XTB's competitive edge versus global fintechs; mitigated via cooperation with university career offices and student organizations and employer participation in training prospective staff (lectures for academic societies).

Low employee loyalty / staff turnover (notably junior-specialist and specialist roles): risks loss of domain knowledge, project delays, higher recruitment/training costs directly burdening financial results; for clients, lower service standards and longer onboarding; market-scale impact on perceived operational stability and corporate governance; elevated process-error exposure in critical AML and KYC areas. Mitigation: optimized recruitment verifying competencies, substantive knowledge and cultural fit; implemented employee adaptation/onboarding process minimizing early-tenure departures; friendly workplace atmosphere; attractive non-wage benefits package; expanded responsibility scope; participation in key decisions; cross-departmental promotion or work in foreign branches.

V. Model risk: potential loss from decisions substantially based on data from internal models, due to errors in model development, implementation or application; drivers include use of incorrect/unreliable/incomplete data, erroneous assumptions or simplifications, and inappropriate tools, techniques or methods (including statistical), plus improper model functioning from inadequate monitoring, validation and updating. Mitigation: register of models used; periodic assessment of their materiality and risk level; validation process for material models.

VI. Business risk: risk of failing to achieve assumed and necessary economic objectives due to failure in market competition. Drivers: adverse change in competitive conditions; client outflow to competitors; low market volatility; low inflation; insufficient responsiveness of costly marketing campaigns; unfavorable economic-social changes. Consequences: higher operating costs or lower revenues from client outflow; financial result below requirements for current/future activity and capital provisioning; revenue decline tied to lower net client deposits, lower client risk appetite, and migration of client investments to low-risk bonds/bank deposits. Mitigation: regular monitoring/analysis of the competitive environment; competitiveness risk-management actions; controlling actions on cost management; ongoing monitoring of trading results; ESG Strategy.

VII. Environmental and climate risks: risk of losses tied to climate change, energy access, extreme weather events, and ESG-requirement adaptation. (a) Physical/climate-change risk affecting business continuity: violent weather events (floods, hurricanes) at Group branch locations threaten staff safety and local office equipment, causing temporary/local client-service disruptions and higher costs, without affecting stability of the main trading platform located in secure zones. Mitigation: geographic office diversification; key IT infrastructure in low-probability extreme-weather locations; immediate remote-work readiness; physical equipment protection; BCP/DR plans and exercises. (b) Energy availability/cost risk (outages and price spikes affecting office and IT upkeep): higher operating costs, supply-interruption risk to service availability, constrained energy-dependent adaptation capacity, and indirect client service disruption. Mitigation: power-continuity contracts/procedures (UPS/aggregation in critical locations); server energy-efficiency optimization; renewable (OZE) energy where feasible; supply cost/risk monitoring. (c) Non-alignment of XTB Group processes with rapidly changing EU and local ESG regulation (CSRD Directive, EU Taxonomy) and non-financial reporting requirements: risk of administrative penalties, regulator sanctions, higher operating costs for legal services and ESG-data audit, and lost credibility with institutional investors increasingly conditioning capital decisions on sustainability metrics. Mitigation: adoption and execution of the ESG Strategy 2024-2027 setting concrete corporate-governance and environmental targets; ongoing legal-environment monitoring; cooperation with external reporting experts; internal control and Compliance system; full carbon-footprint calculation enabling precise emissions reporting/management. (d) Negative market assessment over insufficient climate action or perceived social irresponsibility (lack of transformation engagement, greenwashing allegations): possible outflow of investors/shareholders, financing difficulties, and loss of clients, especially younger generations valuing environmental criteria. Mitigation: publication of the Sustainability Statement and climate policy; purchase of renewable (OZE) energy confirmed by Guarantees of Origin for the Warsaw headquarters; promotion of rational resource management, document digitalization, and responsible e-waste management.

Risk methodology: individual risk factors are assigned to categories per their characteristics and classified by materiality into permanently material, material, or immaterial. Permanently material risks: currency risk, commodity-price risk, instrument-price risk (market-risk category), technological and technical risk, legal risk (operational-risk category), and counterparty risk (credit-risk category) - deemed by Company authorities to continuously and materially affect results given business specificity, complexity, resources, and operations. Other risks are assessed for material loss or serious disruption; deemed material if one materiality criterion is met: materialized material loss in XTB operations; stress-test results indicating high losses under a scenario realizing the risk; recognition as material by the Risk Committee (composed of XTB Supervisory Board members); or business units deeming the risk critical in the risk self-assessment process.
