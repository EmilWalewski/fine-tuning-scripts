Dom Development Group’s 2025 employee agenda focused on labour-law compliance, safe working conditions and management of workforce impacts through HR-led monitoring, preventive actions, operational data, dialogue and listening mechanisms. The Group addressed burnout, overtime and work-life imbalance through training and webinars supporting organizational culture, including in-person sessions on time management, delegation, assertiveness, communication, coping with difficult situations, emotional intelligence and emotion management, plus webinars titled “Design Your Happiness” and “On Men’s Emotions.” Employee wellbeing initiatives also included participation in running events such as Bieg Chomiczówki, Bieg SGH and Poland Business Run, a sports challenge converting physical-activity points into financial support for an employee-selected charity, and an additional day off for all employees on 18 April. Health and safety activities included ergonomics training, first-aid training, fire marshal training and Health Days. Group companies are implementing requirements under the Act on Ensuring Accessibility for Persons with Special Needs and preparing for the Pay Transparency Directive. Effectiveness of employee-related actions is tracked through indicators such as turnover, training-needs analysis and Opinion Barometer results, but the Group has not yet set measurable targets for material employee matters identified in the DMA analysis because HR processes are still being standardized and automated; medium-term 2030 targets are to be announced after analytical work is completed, while current policy effectiveness is monitored continuously and Human Capital Management Strategy actions will continue. Own-workforce data cover all employment-contract employees as of 31 December 2025 and come from external employee-record systems consolidated at Group level; average 2025 employment in the consolidated financial statements was 713.25 FTE. At year-end 2025 the Group employed 713.25 FTE, including 449.84 women and 263.41 men. Permanent employees totaled 633.95 FTE and temporary employees 79.30 FTE, while full-time employees totaled 695.00 FTE and part-time employees 18.25 FTE; 97% of employees worked full-time. Women accounted for 399.54 permanent, 50.30 temporary, 436.00 full-time and 13.84 part-time employees, while men accounted for 234.41 permanent, 29.00 temporary, 259.00 full-time and 4.41 part-time employees. The Group operates only in Poland’s four largest agglomerations and has no employees outside its country of operation. By location, Warsaw had 368.23 employees, including 344.48 permanent, 23.75 temporary, 361.00 full-time and 7.23 part-time; Tricity had 146.49 employees, including 119.69 permanent, 26.80 temporary, 138.00 full-time and 8.49 part-time; Wrocław had 115.70 employees, including 103.70 permanent, 12.00 temporary, 115.00 full-time and 0.70 part-time; and Kraków had 82.83 employees, including 66.08 permanent, 16.75 temporary, 81.00 full-time and 1.83 part-time. In 2025 Group companies hired 80.75 FTE employees, including 54.65 FTE women, representing 67.68% of hires, and terminated contracts with 77.73 FTE employees. The turnover rate was 10.90% in 2025, down from 12.35% in 2024, calculated on exits outside the Group, excluding internal employment changes and excluding people who left on 31 December 2025.
