Excerpt from the Consolidated Sustainability Report (Section 3, Phase 2: Identification of Key Impacts) within the Management Report on the Activities of Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group for 2025 (Grupa Kapitałowa Dom Development S.A., pp. 94-95). Analysis of material risks, impacts and opportunities extended beyond the Group's core construction and real estate activities to also cover the credit-intermediation sector and the serviced-apartments sector. Three business-decision-defined time horizons were adopted: short-term (business perspective = financial reporting period, 12 months); medium-term (business-model perspective, corresponding to investment planning, execution and sales duration, 2-3 years); and long-term (planning perspective, above 3 years). Given the industry's specificity, any horizon beyond three years is always treated as long-term, extending past the standard investment steps (planning, execution, sales period); the 3-year horizon does not exempt Group companies from addressing climate risks, building life-cycle, or circular-economy (GOZ) implementation, with planned actions in these areas still deployed within a maximum medium-term (three-year) window. These horizons informed the risk study and financial materiality assessment. In 2025 the Group conducted a review of its double-materiality analysis results and a partial revision of the process; the next process revision is planned for 2026. A top-down approach was applied: the Group first analyzed topics from the ESRS 1 AR16 list (the starting point for identifying material matters), supplemented it with Group-specific sustainability matters (based on preliminary analyses), then mapped possible impacts, risks and opportunities to those matters. Impact and risk assessment addressed the pre-mitigation (gross) stage. After analyzing the ESRS 1 Annex A material-topics table, topics not relevant to the Group's activities were excluded, following analysis of the business model, business strategy and value chain at the preliminary stage by ESG-responsible personnel. The materiality working team included parties with the best available knowledge of due-diligence processes, notably the Director of Risk and Compliance Management, who drew on existing corporate due-diligence procedures when assessing ESG matters. Preliminary impact-identification analyses were based on: (a) the Group's prior materiality matrices published in non-financial reports and built on the GRI (Global Reporting Initiative) standard; (b) sectoral SIN (Non-Financial Information Standards) materiality matrices; (c) matters established as material within existing due-diligence processes; (d) publicly available sectoral and industry materiality indices; and (e) analysis of scientific publications and literature on trends in the Group's sectors. A peer-comparison analysis of similar companies was also performed for both sectors; in 2025 this competitor analysis was conducted for the second time, based on material topics disclosed in peers' ESRS-based non-financial reports. The due-diligence assessment incorporated procedures functioning in the Group under, among others, the ESG DOM 2030 Strategy, environmental decisions issued by administrative bodies, the Code of Conduct, GK Dom Development Policies, and related implementation actions. Materiality-support tools used included SASB Materiality Finder and the MSCI ESG Industry Materiality Map. The study further drew on: (a) 2024 qualitative interviews with 60 members of Group management; (b) 2024 external interviews with 6 experts (covering architect education in sustainability and climate change, sustainable construction, building carbon-footprint calculation, market conditions, circular economy, and sustainable design); (c) 2024 external interviews with 10 principal Contractors; (d) 2025 analysis of 32 surveys completed by Group employees; (e) 2025 results of a meeting with 10 representatives of expert organizations, ESG bodies and NGOs, plus 3 surveys and a note completed afterward; (f) 2025 customer survey results (119 completed surveys); and (g) 2025 local-community survey results (16 completed surveys). The Group considered impacts in which its companies are involved through their activities or business relationships; part of the material impacts were already reflected in the ESG DOM 2030 Strategy and the ongoing operations of Group companies. On climate-change topics, the Group reviewed its resources, operational processes and value chain to determine climate-related impacts, risks and opportunities. This analysis covered own operations (review of construction and investment-design processes for climate-change adaptation, climate-change mitigation including greenhouse-gas emission reduction, and energy consumption) and the upstream value chain (analysis of suppliers and producers of building materials and equipment regarding their capacity to deliver low-emission materials). For physical climate-risk assessment, the locations where the Group operates were analyzed, with location being the sole differentiating factor; disaggregation therefore applies only to the climate-change-adaptation impact, and no other factors affecting the materiality assessment were identified.
