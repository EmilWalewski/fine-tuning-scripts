Consolidated sustainability reporting excerpt for Dom Development S.A. Capital Group (Management Board Report on the Activities of Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group in 2025), covering ESRS S1-16 remuneration metrics and S1-17 human rights incidents, complaints, and severe impacts.

S1-16 Remuneration Metrics: The gender pay gap is defined as the difference in average remuneration level between female and male employees, calculated as the sum of pay components disbursed in 2025 converted to full-time equivalent and divided by annual hours in 2025 (estimated from working days in the calendar year and an 8-hour workday). Calculations include only employees on employment contracts who worked in Group companies throughout 2025; this group is uniformly subject to the Labour Code and to identical tax and social security (ZUS) rules, whereas persons cooperating under other employment forms have differentiated tax/insurance situations and rates, so including all employees regardless of contract form would materially distort and falsify the picture of pay differences. Pay components comprise: base salary (sum of guaranteed, invariable cash remuneration); cash benefits (sum of cash supplements such as annual, semi-annual and discretionary bonuses, awards, allowances, commissions and other variable cash payments); and in-kind benefits such as private life insurance and healthcare. Management levels are defined as: operational level (non-managerial positions from the assistant, specialist, expert job families); managerial level (managerial positions with or without a subordinate team, plus director positions without a subordinate team); and senior management level (director positions with a subordinate team).

Ratio of average female remuneration to male remuneration by category (gender pay gap):
- Senior management: 2025 = 11.11%, 2024 = 19.77% (improvement, gap narrowed).
- Managerial level: 2025 = 16.46%, 2024 = 17.26% (slight narrowing).
- Operational level: 2025 = -0.74%, 2024 = -2.78% (negative figures, gap narrowed).
- Total/Overall: 2025 = 15.51%, 2024 = 16.99% (year-over-year narrowing of the overall gap).

The metric was not assured by an external body other than the assurance service provider. The pay ratio of the annual total combined remuneration of the highest-paid person in the organization to the median annual total remuneration of all employees on employment contracts (excluding the highest-paid person) was 26.97 in 2025, down from 39 in 2024; the year-over-year change was driven by changes in the Management Board and the remuneration of the highest-earning person. Underlying information is disclosed in notes 7.34 and 7.35 of the consolidated financial statements (SSF).

S1-17 Human Rights Incidents, Complaints and Severe Impacts: An internally implemented Whistleblower Reporting and Protection Procedure (Procedura Zgłaszania Nadużyć i Ochrony Sygnalistów) permits anonymous reporting of all irregularities in the Company. Procedures in selected Group companies comply with applicable regulatory requirements and allow reporting of all types of irregularities, including corruption-related ones, defining conduct rules for both the reporting person and the relevant Group company. Reports may be made, including anonymously in described cases, via dedicated e-mail boxes (etyka@domd.pl, etykadc@domd.pl, etykaddsa@domd.pl, etykaddw@domd.pl, etykaddkw@domd.pl, etykaddk@domd.pl, and etykaES@eurostyl.com.pl), traditional correspondence, telephone, or in person. Suspicions may also be reported openly to the direct superior along the chain of command, and in special cases directly to the President of the Management Board, or alternatively to the team responsible for risk analysis and compliance in a given Group company via riskandcompliance@domd.pl or riskandcompliance@eurostyl.com.pl (by e-mail, telephone, or other commonly used means). The Group's Chief Risk and Compliance Officer remains available to staff members in every situation.

Each report, regardless of transmission method, initiates an internal investigation; the investigating team is always impartial, independent, and unconnected to the reporter. A good-faith report by a whistleblower precludes any retaliatory action, and the Company takes no action to establish the identity of anonymous reporters; for official reports made via the service chain, Group companies provide support and identity protection. Whistleblowing data is maintained in an internal Group register and is not validated by external entities.

In 2025, two complaints were recorded through problem-reporting channels by persons belonging to the entity's own workforce (including grievance mechanisms). Neither concerned discrimination, including harassment, nor human rights violations (relating to breaches of principles in the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, the ILO Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work, and the OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises). No severe human rights incidents involving Group employees were recorded. No penalties, fines, or compensation for damages resulting from reports were imposed on the Group.
