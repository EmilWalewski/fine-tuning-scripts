As of 31 December 2025, KRUK Group’s long-term financial liabilities totaled PLN 6,926.787 M, up from PLN 6,352.006 M at 31 December 2024, driven by secured credits and loans of PLN 3,619.895 M, unsecured debt securities of PLN 3,228.255 M, and lease liabilities of PLN 78.637 M. Short-term financial liabilities totaled PLN 300.223 M versus PLN 274.545 M a year earlier, comprising secured credits and loans of PLN 42.827 M, unsecured debt securities of PLN 232.635 M, and lease liabilities of PLN 24.761 M. During 2025 the Group entered into new leases, particularly for the Parent’s new headquarters and for software, increasing right-of-use assets in property, plant and equipment and in other intangible assets. KRUK does not expect IBOR reform to materially affect financial liabilities, although the final impact remains uncertain because not all system and regulatory solutions have been completed; it is preparing for possible WIBOR cessation by monitoring benchmark regulations, negotiating changes to master and credit agreements for hedging instruments and bank loans, and aligning fallback clauses in new bond issues with market standards and National Working Group recommendations. The Group’s interest-rate risk policy defines objectives, management rules, accepted earnings impact, measurement and monitoring methods, breach procedures, and hedging principles. KRUK manages interest-rate risk through IRS and CIRS instruments: at 31 December 2025 it held open IRS contracts totaling EUR 432.500 M to hedge EURIBOR 1M and 3M cash-flow volatility and EUR credit and bond interest payments, while open CIRS/CCIRS contracts totaled PLN 2,430.000 M. No new CIRS contracts were entered into in Q4 2025. The CCIRS portfolio involved ING Bank Śląski, Santander Bank Polska, DNB Bank ASA, and Alior Bank with KRUK S.A., using WIBOR 3M or WIBOR 3M plus margin on the PLN floating leg and fixed EUR rates of 1.21% to 6.19%, with maturities from January 2026 to June 2031. Key 2025 additions included Santander PLN 100.000 M, DNB PLN 200.000 M, ING PLN 200.000 M, and Santander PLN 100.000 M transactions maturing between 2030 and 2031. These CIRS transactions are under hedge accounting and are intended to convert floating WIBOR 3M exposure into fixed-rate exposure while also hedging FX risk associated with EUR-denominated net assets.
