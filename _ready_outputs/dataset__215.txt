XTB S.A. Consolidated Annual Report 2025, risk-management section, detailing interest-rate sensitivity, credit risk, and market risk exposures with mitigation controls. Interest-rate risk: the Group analyzes sensitivity of pre-tax profit/(loss) to parallel ±50 basis-point shifts in interest rates over a 12-month horizon ending 31.12.2025, reflecting fair-value impact of rate changes on interest-bearing assets. Disclosed sensitivity figures (tys. PLN): a +50 bp move yields +12.9M PLN (12,879 tys.) and a -50 bp move yields -12.9M PLN ((12,879) tys.) on one exposure base; a second base shows +50 bp = -0.033M PLN ((33) tys.) and -50 bp = +0.033M PLN (33 tys.). (Note: source table is partially flattened/orphaned; period and metric mapping for the +50/-50 bp pairs is clear but the precise asset-base label per row is ambiguous, so figures are reported at source magnitude without forced relabeling.)

Credit risk defined as unexpected default or deterioration in creditworthiness threatening obligation performance. Key scenarios and controls: (1) Bankruptcy of a bank holding the Group's or clients' cash — would impair liquidity, force lengthy recovery from bankruptcy estate (with risk of partial/no recovery), trigger client claims for sums exceeding guaranteed-deposit-scheme coverage, and in extreme cases force XTB insolvency. Mitigants: qualitative bank vetting before account opening (market position, financial resources, reputation, equity size, ability to meet/repay obligations; e-banking service quality including operational reliability, liquidity, internet-access speed, security standards, account functionality including investment features; banking-diversification requirement; credit quality; legal system and market practices for client cash custody); minimum required external-agency bank credit ratings plus an early-warning insolvency-probability threshold; ongoing credit-quality monitoring of open accounts; concentration limits curbing excessive client/own-cash concentration in a single capital group. (2) Loss of securities held on XTB accounts due to custodian (depozytariusz) malfunction where client financial instruments prove non-existent — XTB pursues recovery from custodian, a time-consuming process with risk of partial/no recovery, exposing XTB to client claims, liquidity/capital deterioration, and in extreme cases inability to meet obligations. Mitigants: restricting custodians to high-reputation, established entities operating under jurisdictions regulating third-party financial-instrument custody and subject to competent supervisory oversight; annual assessment of the custodian and its designated sub-custodians (credit quality, internal/external securities-custody regulations). (3) Dependence/lack of diversification or excessive exposure to a single entity or related entities — risk of hours/days of restricted access to cash in a given bank or capital group during technical problems; in prolonged financial distress or bankruptcy of an entity/group holding material own or client cash, liquidity disruption and capital problems, extreme-case inability to settle obligations. Mitigants: concentration limit on own funds within one capital group; monitoring of exposures and per-entity credit quality.

Market risk: XTB operates as a market-maker, acting as counterparty to client-opened transactions, so financial results depend on open positions in individual financial instruments and market parameters. (a) Sharp price moves and price gaps in underlying instruments can suddenly revalue XTB's open position causing material financial loss, raise client liabilities (with liquidity pressure if clients mass-withdraw funds), and abruptly increase capital requirements necessitating position hedging; hedging disruptions are more probable during sharp moves; clients face elevated rapid-loss risk (especially at high leverage) via position-value collapse and stop-out closures from insufficient margin. Mitigants: limits on clients/instruments/orders; automated exposure monitoring; triggering of hedging transactions once limits are exceeded. (b) Volatility and volatility-regime-change risk — volatility is the economic rationale for CFD (contracts-for-difference) trading and underlies XTB's business model, but can increase potential market-risk loss, raise internal-capital requirements (VaR-based) requiring position hedging, drive larger period-to-period earnings fluctuations, and increase hedging costs through higher margin maintenance at liquidity providers. Mitigants: VaR methodology, sensitivity analyses, stress tests; dynamic adjustment of risk parameters (limits, exposure, hedging rules). (c) Basis/imperfect-hedging risk — hedging a market-risk-exposed position via transactions on a similar but non-identical underlying can cause loss from divergence between the client-transaction instrument valuation and the hedging instrument. Mitigants: hedging-instrument selection rules; limits on instruments and asset classes; correlation and stress-scenario sensitivity analysis.
