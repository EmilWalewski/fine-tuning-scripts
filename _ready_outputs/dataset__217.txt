Dom Development S.A. Capital Group H1 2025 financial review (Management Board report, approved 27 August 2025). The interim condensed consolidated financial statements were prepared on a historical cost basis under IAS 34 (Interim Financial Reporting), on a going-concern basis, with disclosures abbreviated or omitted versus annual IFRS (EU-endorsed) statements; they are unaudited but reviewed by an independent auditor and should be read together with the audited consolidated statements for the year ended 31 December 2024. Functional and presentation currency is the Polish zloty (PLN), with figures in thousands. The Company and its subsidiaries operate exclusively in Poland; all investments proceeded on or ahead of schedule, and the Board identified no going-concern threats. A separate interim condensed standalone statement of Dom Development S.A. for the six months ended 30 June 2025 was likewise prepared, unaudited and independently reviewed.

Consolidated income statement (H1 2025 vs H1 2024): sales revenue 1,293.9M PLN, down 6% from 1,369.6M PLN; gross profit on sales 455.3M PLN, up 4% from 438.8M PLN; operating profit (EBIT) 302.0M PLN, up 6% from 285.8M PLN; net profit 245.6M PLN, up 6% from 231.2M PLN; basic earnings per share 9.52 PLN versus 8.96 PLN, up 6%. The 6% revenue decline reflected differences in the mix of developments and units handed over (in both average sale price and gross margin) and fewer handovers: 1,595 units delivered in H1 2025 versus 1,637 in H1 2024. Handovers proceed on schedule; most projects conclude in the second half of the year, concentrating most handovers in that period. Revenue derived mainly from finished-goods (completed dwellings); other sales (services and goods) represented 6% (H1 2025) and 7% (H1 2024) of total sales. Significant handover contributions came from the popular segment — Osiedle przy Ryżowej and Osiedle Urbino (Warsaw), Braniborska 80 and Osiedle Międzyleska (Wrocław), 29. Aleja (Kraków) — and the premium segment — Apartamenty Literacka (Warsaw) and Doki (Tri-City). Gross margin on sales rose to 35.2% from 32.0%; operating profit margin reached 23.3%. Late in Q2 2025 the first interest-rate cut in over eighteen months, alongside falling WIBOR, improved mortgage availability and visibly revived developer sales offices, supporting purchase decisions and primary-market activity, though Polish mortgages remain among the EU's most expensive. The Group's activity is not strictly seasonal; inter-quarter fluctuations track construction cycles and the concentration of handovers shortly after occupancy permits. Under IFRS 15, revenue is recognized upon handover of a unit, so reported results do not fully reflect the Group's current position; accordingly the Group also reports current sales volume based on preliminary agreements and paid reservation agreements net of cancellations (detailed in section 2.2).

Consolidated balance sheet: asset structure remained dominated by current assets. Inventories totaled 4,756.3M PLN, representing 79% of the Group's record total assets of 5,992.2M PLN as of 30 June 2025 — 13% higher than year-end 2024. The increase stemmed mainly from inventory growth of 455.1M PLN and cash growth of 374.8M PLN. The Group maintained high liquidity, holding cash of 735.6M PLN, plus 134.3M PLN on open residential escrow accounts (presented within short-term financial assets). The Group's asset position in H1 2025 was very strong and continued to distinguish it favorably within the sector.
