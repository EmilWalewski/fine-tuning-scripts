Narrative notes Polish tax regulations (VAT, CIT, social security) are subject to frequent change, inconsistent interpretation, scarce precedent, and legal ambiguity, creating divergent views among state authorities and between authorities and enterprises; tax settlements and other areas (customs, foreign-exchange) may be subject to inspection with high penalties, fines, and interest, making Polish tax risk higher than in mature systems. Despite a very prudent Group tax policy, amounts presented/disclosed may change following final tax-authority decisions. From 15 July 2016 the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) was introduced into the Tax Ordinance to prevent artificial legal structures created to avoid tax in Poland; GAAR defines tax avoidance as actions taken primarily to obtain a tax benefit contrary to the object and purpose of the tax law, denying the benefit where the conduct was artificial. Indicators of artificial actions include (i) unjustified splitting of operations, (ii) engaging intermediary entities lacking economic/business justification, (iii) mutually offsetting/compensating elements, and (iv) similar actions; the new rules require materially greater judgment in assessing transaction tax consequences. GAAR applies to transactions executed after its entry into force and to pre-effective transactions for which benefits continued to be obtained thereafter, enabling Polish tax authorities to challenge taxpayer arrangements such as group restructuring and reorganization. Segment reporting (7.33): Group activity is substantially homogeneous, principally construction and sale of residential and commercial (service) units and related supporting activities. The parent company operates only on the Warsaw market, while the Group operates additionally on the Tri-City, Wrocław, and Kraków markets, the latter three via subsidiaries.
