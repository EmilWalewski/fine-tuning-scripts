Excerpt from the Management Board Report on the activities of Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group in 2025 (Section 1, Basic Information on the Group), page 31, presenting the Group's qualitative risk register across multiple operational domains; no financial figures, shareholder, board, or tax data are disclosed.

Counterparty relations risk: the Group runs a structured subcontractor contracting process managing identified risks via contractual safeguards against non-performance or improper performance; defined contract acceptance and analysis processes impose restrictive requirements set in documents agreed with counterparties, including a Code of Conduct binding the counterparties of Group companies. Contracting includes counterparty verification in publicly available registers and careful subcontractor selection. The Group recognizes risk of limited subcontractor availability and additional construction-contract risks: potential litigation with subcontractors, obligation to remedy defects or faults, payment of contractual penalties, and contract withdrawal.

Information security risk (loss of confidentiality, availability, or integrity of data): defined as one of the Group's key risks, driven by digitalization of service and construction processes, with Polish companies particularly exposed to cyberattacks. Objective is confidentiality, integrity, and availability of operationally processed data, including stakeholders' personal data. Loss of confidentiality entails unauthorized disclosure of client and counterparty data, potentially triggering legal sanctions and reputational damage. Integrity breaches may lead to erroneous business decisions, contractual disputes, and disrupted reporting. Loss of system/information availability threatens uninterrupted operations, with possible sales interruptions, investment delays, and additional operating costs. Mitigation: appropriate standards, procedures, access controls, backups, and continuous employee awareness. Physical security risk centers on unauthorized access, mitigated by access control processes and office-space work rules. Risk of damage from random events and building infrastructure failures (fire, flooding, installation failures, power outages) causing temporary loss of availability is mitigated via technical inspections and installation servicing, fire and alarm systems, and evacuation plans.

Finance and accounting risk: the key risk is loss of financial liquidity stemming from high capital intensity of projects, delays in unit sales, delayed client payments, and possible interruptions in bank financing; mitigated through detailed cash-flow planning, diversification of funding sources, use of escrow accounts, and strict bank supervision over the release of credit tranches. Risk of incorrect valuation of projects, costs, and revenues may distort financial results and management decisions; mitigated by applying proper accounting standards, internal controls in the month/year-end closing process, and ongoing budget supervision. The Group also defines risks of fund embezzlement and possible reporting and tax errors, including in public-law obligations, limited via appropriate segregation of duties across selected finance-accounting functions, standardization of area policies and procedures, and periodic tax reviews and audits.

Litigation and corporate governance risk: stakeholders may bring claims against Group entities, potentially adversely affecting the Group's financial situation or image regardless of claim merit. Corporate-services risks include failure to observe judicial and administrative deadlines and lack of substantive planning of court proceedings, mitigated by structured legal-services processes.

Risk management, compliance, and internal audit risk: most significant risks include non-compliance of internal procedures and business processes with applicable law, regulatory guidelines, and best market practices, and lack of a coherent Group-wide risk and compliance management system, which may cause legal or industry-regulation breaches or conflicts of interest leading to financial loss or reputational damage; mitigated via a uniform Group-level risk and compliance management policy and an effective irregularity-reporting system. Risk of omitting key risks (ineffective risk management) is mitigated through a coherent Group risk methodology, regular review and update of risk registers, and formal assignment of risk owners. Internal-audit risks (undetected material irregularities and abuses) are mitigated by audit-function independence, risk-based audit planning, close cooperation of audit with compliance and risk management, and monitoring of audit-recommendation implementation. Corporate-insurance risks are addressed through analysis of risk transfer of financial liability to insurers.

Investor relations and stock-exchange services risk: breach of disclosure obligations (notably regarding inside information, current and periodic reports) may result in high administrative penalties, personal liability of Management Board members, and lasting reputational harm on the capital market; mitigated via transparent procedures, staff training, and close cooperation with relevant organizational units.

Serviced-apartments area risk: key operational risks linked to high client rotation, including possible equipment damage, accidents, technical failures, or theft of property; mitigated by stay and facility regulations, careful client communication, and regular technical inspections of units. Financial risks tied to service pricing are limited via active price management, diversification of sales channels, and occupancy monitoring.
