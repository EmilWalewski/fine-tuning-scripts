Dom Development S.A. Capital Group 2025 Management Report, consolidated sustainability statement, climate risk analysis section. Transition risk mitigation measures enumerated: strengthened cooperation/dialogue with value-chain participants; promotion and progressive deployment of low-emission solutions and materials; formation of sectoral/cross-sectoral partnerships promoting low-emission solutions; deployment of solutions reducing demand for electricity, heat, and water; project-level material-management plans to eliminate waste. Identified potential financial impacts of transition risk: increased CAPEX; reduced demand for high-emission products/services causing revenue loss; higher production costs from changing input prices (energy, water) and output costs (waste processing); higher costs from purchasing advanced low-emission equipment/systems, converting/adapting business processes, and premature depreciation of high-emission equipment/materials; increased R&D investment/expenditure on new-technology implementation; costs of climate-adaptation solutions and meeting changing customer preferences.

Technological-change risks: business digitalization and associated risks (cybersecurity, business-continuity assurance, unauthorized data access); IT systems failing to adapt to business/regulatory needs; requirement to incorporate changed heat-supply methods including renewable energy sources (RES/OZE) in investments; replacement of existing materials, equipment, and fleet with lower-emission options; requirement to use lower-emission materials toward zero-emission buildings; engagement in innovations such as power purchase agreements (PPAs) for long-term RES use; potential shortening of asset life cycles with earlier impairment/write-off of high-emission machinery, equipment, and fleet; rising costs from potential full fleet electrification. Market and legal/regulatory risks: dynamics/quality of legal change; dependence of debt financing on ESG regulation; dependence of company financial position on overall industry condition; changing market conditions tied to rising supply; loss of financial liquidity; failure or too-slow satisfaction of customer preferences; rising raw-material, material, and service costs; rising electricity and heat prices. Stated mitigations: assessing profitability of development projects using low-emission materials/technologies; providing low-emission-design training; price-sensitivity assessments and contingency plans for central procurement; developing capacity to meet customer needs and improve value-chain cooperation; diversifying product/material/technology portfolio and broader low-emission sourcing/circular-economy options; reviewing insurance arrangements and monitoring insurance-market changes; assessing exposure to physical climate risk for assets/projects near waterways and coasts; increasing facility resilience to extreme weather via improved protections and adaptive solutions; considering development-project locations; conducting customer-preference (NPS) research; signing long-term agreements with multiple external suppliers.

Physical climate risk analysis based on Polish 21st-century climate-change scenarios prepared by IOŚ-PIB under the Klimada 2.0 project, scenario RCP 8.5 versus the 2011-2020 decade (assuming continued current GHG-emission growth) and Municipal Adaptation Plans (source klimada2.ios.gov.pl, access date 01.02.2024). Acute (extreme-weather) risks include greater probability of local flooding from intermittent/torrential rainfall, increased rainfall and cloudbursts (annual precipitation total and number of days with precipitation >20mm), flash-flood probability in cities, increased frequency of combined-sewer storm overflows and effluent volumes, higher stormwater/combined-sewage pumping costs, contaminated surface runoff, infrastructure damage (e.g., flooded transformer stations), rising groundwater levels, sewer-network failure, and impaired building waterproofing/dampness. Potential financial impacts: impairments and early asset withdrawal due to property damage; reduced asset-insurance availability in high-risk locations; higher insurance premiums from greater adaptation spending and stricter policy terms; reduced revenue from production drops or supply-chain interruptions; reduced revenue/higher costs from adverse employee impacts (health, safety, absenteeism); higher property taxes (cleanup/mitigation); higher margins from increased risk. Mitigations: investments in blue infrastructure and nature-based solutions; best-practice water-management instruments; climate-resilient construction technology; increased microretention (small ponds/reservoirs); preventive/awareness workplace actions; proper material storage.

Per scientific studies, Warsaw, Kraków, and Wrocław face long-term increases in days with extremely high temperature (Tmax ≥25°C), most pronounced in Wrocław—6 more such days in 2050 than the current decade—contributing to longer rain-free high-temperature periods and intensified urban heat-island effects. Further physical risk: rising mean annual precipitation (strongest in Kraków) and days with precipitation >20mm, increasing probability of river flooding in Warsaw, Kraków, and Wrocław; coastal flooding in the Tri-City; and urban flash floods in the Tri-City. The Group used national climate forecasts to perform climate-risk analysis to 2050 for specific operating locations, mapping hazards by asset/operation to assess exposure and sensitivity. All Group operations and assets are located solely in Poland, with locally differentiated climate-risk exposure; most exposed assets are those on floodplains. Regarding pollution topics, the Group reviewed its locations and operational processes to determine pollution-related impacts, risks, and opportunities.
