Excerpt from Section 3 (Consolidated Sustainability Reporting) of the Management Board Report on the activities of Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group for 2025, covering climate risk analysis. The Group, drawing on available national climate-change projections, assessed which physical and transition climate risks may arise at specific operating locations and how they may affect assets. The sole identified opportunity is an extended construction-investment window resulting from milder winters and higher temperatures. The climate risk analysis was conducted over a time horizon to 2050. Since 2021 the Group has measured the emissions of its activity (Scope 1 and Scope 2) and set a reduction target for 2030, forming the basis for managing transition risk.

Methodology comprised: (1) climate risk analysis—identification of physical risks based on 21st-century climate-change scenarios for Poland prepared by IOŚ-PIB under the Klimada 2.0 project, RCP 8.5 scenario, benchmarked against the 2011–2020 decade, plus Municipal Adaptation Plans for Gdańsk, Kraków, Warszawa and Wrocław; and identification of transition risks (regulatory, market, technological); (2) greenhouse-gas emissions assessment—analysis of the Group's Scope 1 and 2 emissions in prior years; (3) supplier-action assessment—summary of building-material suppliers' actions on emission reduction and supply of low-emission construction materials; (4) sector-regulatory benchmarking—comparison of Group companies' actions against best practices in the real-estate and construction industry to identify climate mitigation and adaptation solutions.

The Group identified multiple threats linked to climate change and to social and governance matters, classified as physical and transition threats, alongside several opportunities. ESG-related risks and opportunities are deemed critical to shaping Group strategy and financial planning. Identified risks were entered into a risk register forming part of the risk-management policy, and mitigation actions were defined. The disclosure presents transition risks via a matrix of risk type, threats, examples of potential financial impacts, and prevention/minimization measures.

Mitigation measures for transition (regulatory) risk: (1) continuous oversight of legal requirements on environmental protection and non-financial reporting; (2) executing investments enabling compliance with all environmental and climate requirements; (3) active participation in industry organizations and technical working groups and commenting on changing regulations; (4) precise delegation of duties and responsibilities for identifying environmental regulatory aspects; (5) oversight of the currency and validity periods of public-administration decisions; (6) a strictly defined procedure where updating or obtaining new permits for investments is required; (7) verification of templates and submission deadlines for environmental reports; (8) ongoing monitoring of regulatory and legislative processes that may affect operations; (9) monitoring of political developments to anticipate the impact of possible changes.

Examples of potential financial impacts of transition risk: (1) increased operating costs (e.g., higher insurance premiums); (2) loss of revenue due to more stringent spatial-planning rules and reduced demand for high-emission products; (3) increased costs from tighter environmental-protection rules, potential litigation, fines and penalties, and growing non-financial reporting requirements—translating into higher land-purchase costs or potential inability to purchase land, underestimation of execution costs as at the land-purchase date, higher investment-execution costs, uncertainty in investment-execution valuations, and greater cost pressure on subcontractors with possible need to increase headcount.

Threat sources—failure to respond to EU-level regulatory changes, in particular: (1) amendments to the European Parliament and Council Directive on the energy performance of buildings; (2) CBAM—indirect upward pressure on material prices (aluminium, steel, cement, ceramics, gypsum, lime); (3) EU ETS from 2026 in the heating sector—indirect impact on heat-energy tariffs; (4) Taxonomy—need to progressively align buildings with TKK (technical screening criteria) requirements; (5) the Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD)—obligation to prevent any potential adverse environmental and social impacts across the value chain; (6) new rules restricting certain transport models with significant impact on company-served infrastructure; (7) insurance-market changes consequent on legal changes stemming from the European Commission Communication "Managing climate risk—protecting people and prosperity."
