Excerpt from Management Report on activities of Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group in 2025 (Section 1, Basic Information), enumerating principal risk factors and Group mitigation posture.

Mortgage credit availability risk: driven by borrowers' lack of creditworthiness (low income, high fixed costs, negative credit history), legal costs tied to Swiss-franc ("frankowicze") litigation, and macroeconomic uncertainty (high interest rates, inflation) prompting bank/client caution. In 2025 mortgage availability rose materially on a series of NBP rate cuts, improved creditworthiness (monthly installment fell ~150-180 PLN per 100k PLN of credit) and reviving demand, yielding record application volumes, especially late-year ahead of the "Bezpieczny Kredyt 2%" program wind-down. Management assesses the Group relatively little exposed given dominance of higher-standard apartments in its offer; 42% of apartments sold by the Group in Q4 2025 were bought wholly with buyers' own funds (cash, no mortgage).

Inflation risk: rising materials and labor costs raise investment costs, compress margins, and may push apartment prices up. End-2025 saw no material change in construction-materials market; per Polskie Składy Budowlane, December year-on-year prices fell slightly, with retail materials cheaper than wholesale, though some categories (timber, OSB boards) clearly rose. Per latest GUS data, construction-assembly production prices in December 2025 rose +3.6% versus December 2024 and +0.5% versus November 2025. Owning in-house general contractors lets the Group optimize the build process from planning stage, leveraging scale and steady cooperation with proven contractors, expected to materially limit unexpected cost-increase risk and preserve satisfactory profitability.

Labor-market risk: labor and housing markets tightly linked; stable/growing employment (low unemployment, wage growth) fuels housing demand and prices, while slowdown depresses demand. Employment-stability expectations are a key purchase determinant, especially for mortgage-backed buyers. Skilled-labor shortages and rising labor costs heighten development risk via higher build costs or extended timelines, potentially forcing margin defense (e.g., curbing new projects) and creating delay/price risks for buyers. Registered unemployed at end-December 2025 totaled 887.9 thousand persons, an unemployment rate of 5.7%, marginally above November's 5.6%; economists deem this typical seasonal effect (winter reduces activity in construction, agriculture, tourism). Long-term outlook optimistic: rate projected to fall to 5.5% in 2026 and 5.35% in 2027, assuming stable economic growth and demographic shifts steadily shrinking labor supply. Management continuously monitors employment and wage data to align offer (size, standard, price) to buyers' real capacity, and mitigates labor shortage via improved employment terms, development/training, succession planning, and engagement-building to sustain business continuity.

Administrative-dependency risk: unforeseen events in official/regulatory processes (permit delays, zoning-plan changes, WZiZT/PnB procedure issues, legislative changes) can delay projects, raise costs, or block investments, directly affecting schedule, budget, and project profitability. Development projects require numerous permits at each investment stage; despite cautious schedules, risk persists of delays, revocation, or non-issuance of decisions. Key mitigation is rigorous project appraisal and ongoing control of developments via established investment models and decision procedures under Management's particular attention. Group maintains adequate scale and smooth offer replenishment, including a land bank corresponding to over 4 years of sales.

External-financing availability risk: development activity demands significant, long-term capital commitment, so the Group relies substantially on external financing; regulatory changes, deteriorating capital markets, and tighter bank credit policy could materially affect its financial position. Mitigated by carefully matching debt maturity structure to business specifics, i.e., reliance on long-term external capital sources. Management assesses exposure low given conservative financial policy; substantial cash holdings and high credibility with financial institutions are sources of competitive advantage and undisturbed investment execution.

Real-estate price risk: profitability depends on Polish apartment price levels, particularly in the Group's operating markets; price declines could prevent sales at favorable prices, materially adversely affecting results. Mitigated by appropriate location selection, operating in Poland's largest cities where housing demand, despite fluctuations, remains highest.
