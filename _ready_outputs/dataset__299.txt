Section 2 of the Management Board report on operations in H1 2025 for the Dom Development S.A. Capital Group covers Legal Regulations, Administrative Decisions, and Operating Markets; qualitative narrative without financial figures.

Legal Regulations. The Board notes regulatory changes effective in H1 2025 and announced prospective changes may directly or indirectly affect the Group's operations and results. Effective 7 May 2025, amendments to the Act on Spatial Planning and Land Development respond to municipalities' practical difficulties meeting deadlines for adopting General Plans (Plany Ogólne). The amendment extends validity of existing municipal land-use studies (studia uwarunkowań i kierunków zagospodarowania przestrzennego) until 30 June 2026, giving local governments more time to complete General Plan procedures without suspending adoption of local zoning plans (miejscowe plany zagospodarowania przestrzennego) or issuance of zoning/development condition decisions to investors. The so-called housing special act "Lex Deweloper" (Act of 5 July 2018 on facilitating preparation and execution of housing investments and accompanying investments) is also extended, ceasing to have effect on 1 July 2026. Other changes enable preparation of first general plans bypassing outdated development strategies and supplement transitional provisions allowing completion of ongoing housing-investment location procedures conducted under the special act. These provisions preserve continuity of design processes and reduce uncertainty over development potential of land held in the Group's land banks and land under acquisition. Sejm proposals to amend the housing special act address the minimum parking-space ratio, which after the last amendment reduced the number of investments executed under that formula. Proposed solutions would replace the current 1.5 spaces per dwelling ratio with options including municipal council setting an individual ratio or removing the ratio entirely from the act. The Board states that maintaining the current minimum parking ratio reduces attractiveness of housing investments under the special-act formula, negatively affects plot capacity of parcels with special-act potential, and raises execution costs.

On 11 July, amendments to the Act of 20 May 2021 on protection of rights of buyers of residential premises or single-family homes and on the Developer Guarantee Fund (the "developer act") took effect. The change compels developers to maintain their own website publishing prices of dwellings offered, requires daily price-information updates, collection and publication of historical price data, and daily reporting of changes to the government system dane.gov.pl. These regulations forced rebuilding of the Group's IT systems and revision of existing sales policy.

In June 2025, the Ministry of Development and Technology submitted for consultation a draft new regulation on technical conditions to be met by buildings and their siting. The Ministry states the regulation will make buildings more resident-friendly, safe, and energy-efficient. Conditions include introducing a "zero-emission" standard for new buildings and requiring technical systems in new buildings to be equipped with devices for automatic control, regulation, and monitoring. The changes stem from entry into force of Directive (EU) 2024/1275 of the European Parliament and Council of 24 April 2024 on the energy performance of buildings, which the Group flagged in prior reports. The draft also contains fire-protection provisions intended to limit certain construction solutions conducive to uncontrolled fire spread. The regulation aims to adopt solutions matching current construction technologies, accelerate and ease investment execution, and provide transparency for investors, removing difficulties from ambiguous provisions that led to differing interpretations by designers and architectural-construction authorities. Clarifications target: definition of an underground storey; width of access and driveways to building plots and buildings; siting of parking spaces and their groupings; application of provisions to historic buildings; and sunlight exposure and obstruction. The Board's assessment remains unchanged: changes will negatively affect investment execution costs, development and land-use potential, reduce the number of dwellings per investment, raise average prices of all units, and consequently lower housing availability.

Administrative Decisions. Development projects require numerous administrative decisions, permits, and consents across stages of the investment process. Despite the Group's cautious scheduling, risks of delays, decision annulment, or refusal cannot be fully eliminated, potentially affecting timeliness of ongoing and planned investments; legislative changes add risk requiring schedule corrections. To ensure operational continuity and a broad, diversified market offering, the Group maintains a high-quality land bank of appropriate scale enabling sales activity for approximately four years. Key risk mitigation includes constant monitoring of legislative changes and their systematic incorporation into schedules and decision-making processes, applying the Group's investment models and decision procedures as an element of corporate governance and risk management.

Operating Markets. Dom Development S.A. has long held leadership on the Warsaw primary housing market; Warsaw, Poland's leading economic, social, and academic center, has high growth dynamics and a strong labor market attracting new residents, making it the country's largest housing market with the highest transaction prices. The Warsaw market poses challenges from limited availability of investment land with regulated legal status, delays in adopting planning documents, and issuing administrative decisions. To reduce concentration risk on the capital market, the Board pursues geographic expansion within the Group. The Group also operates in Tricity (Trójmiasto), Wroclaw, and Krakow—Poland's four most developed housing markets alongside Warsaw. In the Board's view, geographic diversification and the rising significance of subsidiaries in the Group's sales structure and financial results form an effective long-term value-building strategy to be continued in coming years.
