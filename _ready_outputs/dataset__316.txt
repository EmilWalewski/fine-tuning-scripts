XTB S.A. Capital Group 2025 Consolidated Annual Report excerpt detailing corporate governance, shareholder rights, and Supervisory Board composition. Supervisory Board election by separate groups: where the Statute mechanism does not apply, shareholders follow the Commercial Companies Code (KSH) procedure; total share count is divided by total number of Supervisory Board members, and shareholders representing that quotient may form a separate group to elect one Board member, forfeiting voting on other members. Unfilled seats after group voting are elected by shareholders who did not join any group. Under separate-group voting, voting-right privilege limitations do not apply and each share carries one vote, excluding shares without voting rights.

Shareholder right to challenge General Meeting resolutions via two actions against the Company: (1) action to set aside a resolution contrary to the Statute or good practice and harming the Company's interest or intended to wrong a shareholder, filed within one month of learning of the resolution but no later than three months from adoption; (2) action to declare nullity of a resolution contrary to statute.

Special-purpose auditor (rewident do spraw szczególnych) provisions: must possess professional knowledge and qualifications ensuring a reliable, objective examination report; cannot be an entity that provided services during the examined period to the Company, its parent, subsidiary, dominant entity, or significant investor per the Accounting Act, nor an entity within the same capital group as such a service provider. Management Board and Supervisory Board must furnish documents specified in the General Meeting resolution or court appointment order plus necessary explanations. The special auditor must submit a written examination report to both Boards; the Management Board transmits it as a current report. The report may not disclose technical, commercial, or organizational secrets unless necessary to justify its conclusions. The Management Board must report on how examination results were addressed at the next General Meeting.

Right to demand appointment of an expert auditor for special matters (Public Offering Act, Art. 84): at the request of shareholder(s) holding at least 5% of total votes, the General Meeting may resolve to examine, at Company cost, a defined issue related to the Company's formation or conduct of affairs. Such shareholders may demand convening an extraordinary General Meeting or inclusion of the matter on the next General Meeting's agenda. If, within two weeks of the convening demand to the Management Board, the extraordinary General Meeting is not convened, the registry court may authorize the requesting shareholders to convene it and appoints its chairman. To place the matter on the next General Meeting's agenda, the demand must be delivered to the Management Board in writing no later than twenty-one days before the planned meeting date. The selection resolution must specify the special auditor (with the applicant's written consent), the examination subject and scope (consistent with the application unless the applicant agreed in writing to changes), document types to be provided, and the examination commencement date no longer than 3 months from resolution. If the General Meeting fails to adopt a resolution consistent with the application or adopts one violating Art. 84(4) of the Public Offering Act, applicants may, within 14 days of adoption, petition the registry court to appoint the indicated entity as special auditor.

Supervisory Board mandates expire on the date of the General Meeting approving financial statements for the last full financial year of the member's term, and in other cases defined in the KSH.
