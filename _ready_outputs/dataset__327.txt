Excerpt from the consolidated sustainability statement within the Management Board Report on the activities of Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group (Grupa Kapitałowa Dom Development S.A.) for 2025, detailing the double materiality assessment methodology. Materiality thresholds for positive actual impact (assessed by scale, scope) are graded: Critical = 10; Significant = 8-9; Important = 5-7; Informational = 2-4; None = 0-1. Materiality thresholds for potential negative impact (assessed by scale, scope, irreversibility, probability) are graded: Critical = 16-20; Significant = 13-15; Important = 9-12; Informational = 6-8; None = 0-5.

Phase 4 (Identification and assessment of risks and opportunities): the Group companies developed risks and opportunities across environmental, social and governance (ESG) domains derived from identified impacts. The double materiality analysis incorporated both financial and non-financial aspects of risks and opportunities, linking them to identified impacts to better understand how external and internal factors affect long-term strategy and the business model. The integrated approach supports resource management, risk minimization, and capture of opportunities to improve financial results, reputation, and business resilience amid changing market conditions. Hierarchy of sustainability risks relative to other risk types is established within the risk management system; despite the typically long-term nature of physical climate risks, the Group incorporated sustainability risks into the overall business risk register (BRM). ESG risks are assessed for probability of occurrence and potential effects, then classified by materiality to strategic objectives, with priority on risks that could negatively affect operations, financial results, or stakeholder relations.

To determine financial materiality, internal assessors (ESG coordinators and risk management/compliance coordinators) rated probability of a given risk/opportunity on a 0-5 scale (0 = event most likely will not occur; 5 = occurrence highly probable) and financial effects on a 0-5 scale (0 = effects immaterial to operations; 5 = catastrophic effects). Probability scale anchors: 0 None; 1 very low/practically impossible; 2 low (unlikely given current practices and procedures); 3 medium (event has occurred under similar circumstances/conditions); 4 high (occurrence probable); 5 very high (occurrence practically certain, may recur multiple times). Financial effects scale anchors: 0 none (immaterial to operations); 1 very small (minimal impact, mitigable by standard measures); 2 small (affects normal project functioning at local scope); 3 medium (serious event requiring additional action with moderate consequences); 4 large (critical event requiring extraordinary action with long-term consequences); 5 very large (catastrophic effects potentially leading to asset liquidation, causing significant damage).

Phase 5 (Approval of material topics by the Management Board of Dom Development S.A.): double materiality analysis results were presented to the Management Board of the parent company for iterative feedback and process validation. The Management Board of Dom Development S.A. approved the selected material sustainability topics to be reported by the Group. Results were also presented to the Audit Committee of the Supervisory Board.

Decision-making process in the double materiality analysis and related internal control procedures (process / actions / responsibility): Scope of analysis - analysis of legal regulations - Non-Financial Reporting Team, Risk & Compliance Team; Identification of impacts, risks and opportunities - preliminary analyses - Non-Financial Reporting Team and Group company management; Impact assessment - assessing materiality of identified issues - Non-Financial Reporting Team, Risk & Compliance Team; Financial assessment - assessing impact of risks and opportunities on financial results using materiality thresholds - Non-Financial Reporting Team, Risk & Compliance Team; Strategic decisions - priority actions affecting strategy and business model, including short-term initiatives (e.g., adjusting operating procedures) and long-term strategies (e.g., investments in renewable/RES energy) - Management Board.

Internal control procedures accompanying double materiality decisions: (1) adoption of internal sustainability policies; (2) documentation of the double materiality risk identification and assessment process; (3) regular verification of compliance of the double materiality analysis with regulatory guidelines and internal standards; (4) internal work on collecting resource consumption data; (5) introduction of rules on regular sustainability training and organizational awareness-building. Business owners of individual areas are responsible for monitoring and managing material impacts, risks and opportunities; these are monitored at least annually for probability and materialization effects, with new risks and opportunities related to investment execution also analyzed. The Group integrated material sustainability matters into its management process; due diligence tools enable ongoing monitoring of effectiveness, supporting identification and assessment of potential negative impacts and risks as well as implementation of effective corrective actions.
