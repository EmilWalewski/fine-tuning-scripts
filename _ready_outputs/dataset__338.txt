Excerpt from the Management Board Report on operations of Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group (Grupa Kapitałowa Dom Development S.A.) for 2025, covering ESG governance disclosures (GOV-3, GOV-4, GOV-5) within the consolidated sustainability reporting. ESG Coordinator: at operational level, the ESG Coordinator is responsible for sustainability activities, reporting organizationally and functionally directly to the Company's Management Board President (CEO) and reporting on sustainability execution and oversight to the Supervisory Board via the Audit Committee; duties include coordinating sustainability projects, collection and processing of non-financial data, and operational oversight of strategy execution and planned ESG initiatives. Management: all senior management (business owners of all Group areas) engage in ESG processes through development of sustainability activities, participation in the sustainability report, and expert roles in projects (e.g., materiality assessment, climate-risk analysis and evaluation); cross-level management communication ensures Group-wide consistency. GOV-3 (sustainability outcomes in incentive systems): in 2025 the Group incorporated sustainability into remuneration of three Management Board members, with 5-15% of these members' variable remuneration contingent on meeting sustainability targets tied to the business area each supervises; presidents of subsidiaries also hold sustainability targets. Targets relate to execution of the ESG DOM 2030 Strategy — implementation of the Urban Greenery Program (Program Miejska Zieleń), completion of taxonomy disclosures, and oversight of ESG Strategy execution, including the greenhouse-gas emissions reduction target. GOV-4 (due-diligence statement): a table maps core due-diligence process elements to sustainability report disclosure points — embedding due diligence in governance, strategy and business model (GOV-2, SBM-3, IRO-1, S1-2); engaging affected stakeholders at all key due-diligence stages (S1-3, S1-14, S1-17, G1-1, G1-2); identifying and assessing adverse impacts (SBM-3, E1-3, E1-4, E3-2, E4-3, E5-2); taking action to mitigate identified adverse impacts (S1-1, S1-3, S1-4, S2, S3, S4, G1, GOV-2); and monitoring effectiveness and communicating results (S1-2, S1-4, S1-17, G1-3). GOV-5 (risk management and internal controls over sustainability reporting): the Supervisory Board oversees the internal control, risk management, compliance-assurance and internal-audit systems across the Group, particularly via the Audit Committee, and presents to the General Meeting an assessment of the Company's situation including adequacy and effectiveness of these systems; sustainability risk management and internal controls are integral to the Group's organizational culture and corporate governance and aligned with the strategy of operational excellence and principles of sustainable, responsible business, translating strategic assumptions into specific operational targets, linking operational management with risk management, assignment of responsibility for operational results, achievement assessment, and support of effectiveness across all organizational levels. Sustainability risk management: compliance risk and selected operational risks are of particular ESG significance; risks material to reporting control concern data completeness, data integrity, estimate accuracy, and timeliness of data delivery. The Group manages these risks per its internally developed Non-financial and Sustainability Reporting Procedure and internal Standard for risk, compliance, audit and quality management — DOM 2030, with the process covering risk identification and assessment (data-source mapping, analysis of data requiring estimates, assessment of probability and impact of errors and delays), control implementation (policies and procedures, internal controls such as data-completeness checklists, cross-validations and verifications, estimation standards, reporting calendar), monitoring and oversight (data-collection progress reports, reporting to the Management Board and Audit Committee), corrective actions (training for data providers, IT system improvements, assignment of roles and responsibilities for report content), and external verification (internal control and cooperation with the statutory auditor). Internal control system: a three-lines-of-defense internal control system (detailed in report chapter 4.11) is implemented Group-wide and operates in Group companies respecting formal separateness, proportionality and adequacy, ensuring operational effectiveness, reliable non-financial reporting, and legal/internal-regulatory compliance; it is based on advanced accounting, business reporting and document-flow systems, a utility-consumption data-collection system, transparent internal (including financial and compliance) procedures, a clear organizational structure, competent personnel, management oversight with regular Group evaluation, verification of financial and non-financial statements by an independent statutory auditor, and internal audit activity.
