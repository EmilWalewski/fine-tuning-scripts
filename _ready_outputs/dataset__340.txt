Dom Development S.A. Group 2025 Management Report, Consolidated Sustainability Statement, disclosure E5-5 "Resources Diverted/Outflows" (waste). Group companies design buildings for maximum durability to reduce renovation frequency and life-cycle environmental burden. Per industry literature and durability standards: reinforced-concrete and mixed structures used by the Group have average durability of 50-100 years; technical installations 20-30 years; finishing materials up to 10 years.

Waste volumes depend largely on the number of active investments, arising mainly during construction and demolition works. Group companies generate waste until obtaining the occupancy permit (pozwolenie na użytkowanie); thereafter waste management falls to the investment's Property Manager (Zarządca). Waste handling complies with the Environmental Protection Law (Prawo ochrony środowiska, Dz. U. 2001 poz. 627) and the Waste Act (Ustawa o odpadach, Dz. U. 2013 poz. 21). Prevention is prioritized via appropriate raw materials, selective collection, and on-site reuse.

In 2025 a construction-waste management campaign was conducted: webinars, subcontractor meetings, visits to waste recipients, and recipient-organized seminars for staff; a recurring collection of used workwear and personal protective equipment was also held. Waste management on sites is planned within the Health and Safety Plan (BIOZ), which includes a Waste Management Plan prepared for each investment. Waste generated during construction is segregated and stored selectively in designated, sealed-base, clearly marked containers; the Group does not store waste, especially hazardous waste. Waste is transferred only to entities holding both collection and processing permits and an active BDO registry number, ensuring reuse and return to the economic circuit.

The largest-volume waste stream is mixed construction, renovation and demolition waste (code 17 09 04); the Group considers its share too high and aims to reduce it via: new segregation requirements for construction/demolition waste (effective 01.01.2025, covering at least wood, metals, glass, plastics, gypsum, and mineral waste including concrete, brick, tiles, ceramics, stone); regular training and inspection of own staff and subcontractors; eventual sanctioning of non-compliance; and reuse of on-site materials (e.g., demolition rubble as building material where parameters permit). Under current legal interpretation all materials removed from sites are waste subject to strict legal procedure; reuse as building materials requires controls and technical compliance. Reported waste originates from demolition and construction processes and contains metals (iron and steel).

The Group participates in Poland's mandatory waste-data collection system; all waste information is continuously monitored and recorded in the waste database (part of the products, packaging and waste-management database). Group companies lack direct information on downstream processing/disposal methods, as waste is transferred to specialist firms. Construction-waste treatment methods are estimated from nationwide data (excluding Dom Construction, Wrocław branch, which holds disposal reports); municipal-waste management is estimated excluding the Wrocław headquarters, which holds primary data. The increase in waste sent to recycling in 2025 stems from implementation of the amended Waste Act.
