Dom Development S.A. Capital Group 2025 Management Report (consolidated sustainability statement, disclosure E5-4 Resources Inflows) details material resources consumed across operations and the value chain. Key non-renewable construction materials used by Group companies on building sites are construction reinforcement, concrete, and silicate and ceramic blocks; steel (construction reinforcement) is classified as a critical resource central to building structure. The Group discloses mass of technical and biological materials used in the reporting period plus the share of secondary and recycled materials used in construction projects and component production. Consumption data is sourced from the Group's internal system, drawn directly from invoices. The Group reports no use of biological materials (including wood and other natural raw materials) in construction, citing limited applicability in multi-family residential building; consequently the cascading use of biological raw materials method does not apply, and materials used do not originate from waste and/or by-products. In 2025 the company purchased 0.3% of materials from recycling (versus 1.11% in 2024); this concerned construction reinforcement, in whose production 92.5% steel scrap was used.

Quality and warranty: all clients are covered by a 5-year warranty (rękojmia) period counted from the date of property ownership transfer, during which the Guarantee Service handles complaint reports; after-sales service and defect remediation may also proceed under 2-year liability for non-conformity with contract regarding movable apartment elements. Group companies pursue ongoing quality improvement of investments; internal standards on technical requirements, based on legal regulations, are updated annually. A long-standing internal investor supervision function operates, with sector inspectors controlling work progress, verifying material quality, and conducting internal audits on every site. The Group corrected 2024 consumption figures for silicate blocks, ceramic blocks, mineral wool, and total material consumption.
