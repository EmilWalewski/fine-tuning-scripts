Excerpt from the Management Board Report on the Activities of Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group in 2025 (Consolidated Sustainability Reporting), covering ESRS S2 (Workers in the Value Chain) and ESRS S3 (Affected Communities) for the Dom Development S.A. Capital Group.

S2 – Workers in the Value Chain: The double materiality assessment identified occupational health and safety (OHS) of value-chain workers as material. Financial assessment of the double materiality analysis flagged risks relating to OHS, employment security, working time, and adequate pay of subcontractors' workers. The Group's business model and strategy address its impact on the work safety of subcontractors' employees, focusing on minimizing hazard risk in the construction industry, regarded as particularly accident-prone. Mitigation is delivered through a system of measures protecting the health and life of persons working on construction sites, including regular audits, training, and verification of compliance with safety standards, which translates into reduced accidents. Each construction site additionally runs OHS training for employees and collaborators and implements preventive procedures such as occupational risk assessments and ongoing monitoring of working conditions. The Group has not set targets or metrics for the topics identified under the DMA and accordingly holds no results of actions in this area. A corporate document material to the Group's supply chain is the Code of Conduct – Contractor Code of Conduct, responding to the Company's environmental and quality requirements imposed on Contractors and supporting verification of the legality of their operations. Its provisions undergo cyclical review for alignment with best market practices and applicable regulations. The Code obliges Contractors, among others, to: comply with contract terms, legal provisions, and confidentiality; observe laws on the environmental impact of their activities; respect, support, and uphold provisions protecting human rights and children's rights as well as international humanitarian law standards; and eliminate and reject all forms of modern slavery, human trafficking, forced labor, compulsory labor, and violations of workers' rights. The Code is grounded in the ten principles of the United Nations Global Compact, the United Nations Guiding Principles for Business and Human Rights (including principles and rights set out in the eight fundamental conventions of the ILO Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work and the International Bill of Human Rights), and the OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises on Responsible Business Conduct. Section S1-1 covers the extent to which the Group's Fundamental Rights Policy applies to actions toward Contractors upon identified breaches; Section S1-14 includes indicators on accidents of value-chain workers (Subcontractors).

S3 – Affected Communities: The double materiality process identified material community-specific disclosures: supporting the development of sustainable cities, and managing relations with the surrounding environment. Group companies pursue these continuously in line with their CSR Strategy and have not set specific, measurable targets for these topics. The business model and strategy address impacts tied to city development and stakeholder relations: engagement in sustainable cities occurs through designing and delivering projects that increase urban attractiveness and cohesion and support economic and social development, offering resident-friendly spaces promoting social integration. Relationship management relies mainly on dialogue with local communities and implementing solutions minimizing nuisances from investments (noise, traffic disruptions). Group companies act for local communities in education, sport, activation of the elderly, and housing, consistent with adopted CSR Strategies. To support these efforts, Fundacja Nasz Dom (Our Home Foundation) commenced operations in 2024.

CSR activities center on three pillars. (1) Space for local communities: the Wrocław company joined the municipal "Cycling Action Plan to 2030," creating new cycling infrastructure in Tarnogaj and Maślice; in Kraków at the Górka Narodowa Estate, stormwater drainage was rebuilt and a retention reservoir constructed. These investments are realized within developer project budgets and are reflected in note 7.35 of the consolidated financial statements under "external services." In 2025, Euro Styl S.A. completed, under a Public-Private Partnership, a public objective at Dolne Miasto (historic Gdańsk midtown), modernizing a pedestrian-cycling route with a linear park along Kamienna Grobla St. (over the New Motława canal). In Warsaw, at the Żoliborz Artystyczny Estate, Dom Development S.A. rebuilt Brandysa St. as a woonerf and built a missing section of Hübnera St.; in Bemowo, in cooperation with other developers, new sections of Człuchowska St. and Heweliusza St. were delivered. Over PLN 10 million was allocated to these road investments. Fundacja Nasz Dom financed three educational-facility modernizations: three cloakrooms, a corridor with connector, and two large bathrooms at Primary School No. 293 (Jan Kochanowski) in Warsaw-Bielany; refurbishment of part of the bathrooms at Primary School No. 7 (Polish Army) in Kraków; and purchase of furniture, modern teaching aids, and multimedia boards for Wrocław's SPECTO Public Primary School. (2) Supporting art and artists: in 2025 Dom Development Wrocław financially supported the CYRKOPOLE new-circus festival (unique in Wrocław) and the EMMA foundation, which launched a community garden for children's ecological education. (3) Helping those in need: the Group has long supported the most needy, especially persons in homelessness crisis, and since the start of the war in Ukraine has supported Ukrainian citizens. Via Fundacja Nasz Dom, the Group continued cooperation with Ukrainian partner Fundacja Housing for IDPs; in 2025 the renovation of an abandoned senior home in the Vinnytsia Oblast was completed, with Fundacja Nasz Dom allocating PLN 3.2 million, creating 14 apartments and an air-raid shelter. Fundacja Nasz Dom also financed in 2025 the renovation of the new premises of SzkoUA – the Warsaw Ukrainian School on Terespolska St.
