Excerpt from the Management Board Report on operations of Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group for 2025 (Grupa Kapitałowa Dom Development S.A.), Section 3 Consolidated Sustainability Reporting, SBM-3: material impacts, risks and opportunities and their interconnection with strategy and business model. The Group tabulates material impacts identified via its materiality assessment, mapped to where they concentrate across the business model, own operations and value chain (upstream/downstream). Three impacts are Group-specific additional disclosures relating to ESRS S3; all others fall under standard ESRS disclosure requirements. The 2025 review of the double materiality analysis identified new impacts associated with: investment on floodplain land increasing buildings' vulnerability to climate change; employee health burden during periods of intensified activity (occupational burnout); and insufficient availability/communication of misconduct (whistleblowing) reporting channels. These were incorporated following workshops with ESG experts. Newly identified risks and opportunities include two opportunities—improved environmental safety and crisis preparedness via rapid-response procedures, and increased material efficiency from building-construction optimization processes—and four risks: floodplain investment location (materializing on total levee failure), professional burnout and attrition of highly qualified staff, sector exposure to forced labor, and inadequate communication of whistleblowing channels. As of report publication date, the Group perceives no new and forecasts no future consequences of material impacts on its business model, value chain, strategy or decision-making; the 2025 identified material impacts, risks and opportunities had no effect on the Group's current financial situation.

The double materiality process identified affected stakeholder categories: employees (full/part-time employment contracts, mandate/commission contracts) and self-employed persons (contractors and specialists conducting business activity providing services to Group companies). Particular attention is given to workers during investment execution, who face the greatest risk of health detriment and require dedicated OHS communication channels. The Group is involved in identified material impacts through its own activity (daily operations: energy and raw-material consumption, environmental emissions, employee/contractor recruitment and development, organizational culture) and via business relationships (suppliers, subcontractors, clients). To address impacts, the Group implements responsible-conduct and value-chain-management policies and procedures, including supplier selection criteria, ESG compliance monitoring/assessment mechanisms, and remedial actions for detected irregularities, plus stakeholder dialogue.

Selected material impacts are embedded in the ESG DOM 2030 Strategy and ongoing operations, covering: climate change mitigation via Scope 1 and 2 emission-reduction actions, with Group companies progressively increasing renewable-energy contracting—lowering long-term operating costs while meeting legal requirements—and incorporating adaptation solutions raising property value; biodiversity impact from direct resource exploitation (tree and shrub felling), mitigated through numerous plantings on new estates and green-area creation under the Miejska Zieleń (Urban Greenery) project; and circular economy (GOZ) impacts via operational digitization and contractor relationships in raw-material use and lawful waste management—in 2025 the Group developed and implemented a Green Procurement Standard (Standard Zielonych Zamówień) enabling circular-economy principles.

Tabulated impact themes (across Design, Construction, own operations, Marketing, Legal, Sales, HR, Risk/Compliance & Audit departments, with short/medium/long-term horizons): resource use reduction via construction optimization and minimized material design; waste generation (construction/demolition and serviced-apartment operations), with the construction and serviced-apartment sector a major waste-stream contributor; employment security favoring permanent staff with development; working time featuring daily overtime despite task-based schedules; adequate pay—average Group remuneration exceeds the average enterprise-sector monthly wage; work-life balance disrupted by workload/overtime and intensification-period burnout; occupational health and safety in an industry regarded as dangerous and accident-prone; equal treatment and equal pay for equal-value work for all genders (Group reported a high pay gap in prior years); training and skill development with annual periodic appraisal including a development conversation; and value-chain workers' working conditions and OHS, where Dom Development influences conditions on construction sites staffed by subcontractor-employed personnel.
