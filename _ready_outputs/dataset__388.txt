Dom Development S.A. Capital Group 2025 consolidated sustainability report (Management Report sections G1-2, G1-3, G1-6) details supplier relationship management, anti-corruption controls, and payment practices.

Supplier relationship management (G1-2): The Group manages supplier relations strategically, weighing cost, quality, and supply-chain risk. Internal regulations govern proper circulation, control, and approval of purchase documents from all suppliers and ensure payment security to correct bank accounts; purchase-document workflow runs through Group companies' financial-accounting systems or a dedicated application, with participants bound to fixed task deadlines. The construction sector is identified as prone to late-payment problems; the Group influences the scale and duration of trade credit (deferred-payment purchasing) from suppliers, with contractual payment-term negotiation always reflecting the Act of 4 November 2022 amending the law on counteracting excessive delays in commercial transactions and the public-finance law, which protects smaller enterprises in asymmetric relations with large-enterprise debtors. The Group has not recognized a need for a separate late-payment-prevention policy, relying on generally applicable law, and has set no metrics in this area. All Group companies require suppliers to observe environmental and social standards in the Contractor Code of Conduct. In 2025 the Group implemented a Sustainable Procurement Standard incorporating environmental and social issues into price negotiations; general contractors Dom Construction and Euro Styl Construction include in their offer-comparison tables ESG-criteria fulfillment (via a prior contractor survey), delivery distance from the investment site, and Code of Conduct compliance. In 2026 Group companies will develop a systematic classification of contractors by enterprise size.

Anti-corruption (G1-3): All Dom Development Group companies adopted an Anti-corruption, Gift, and Conflict-of-Interest Policy (detailed in G1-1). In 2025 no anti-corruption/bribery training was conducted because the Group's training program schedules such training for 2026; Management Board and Supervisory Board members did not attend such training in 2025. Each staff member (via the internal Anti-corruption Handbook) and business partners (via the Code of Conduct) must familiarize themselves with anti-corruption rules covering staff, partners, public-administration cooperation, gift acceptance/giving, and conflict-of-interest avoidance. A corruption-threat analysis found no areas free of corruption-risk probability, so training programs will target all staff; the Group also provides educational materials and one-pagers. Contractors must comply with Code of Conduct anti-corruption provisions (no corrupt acts, lawful transaction settlement, conflict-of-interest and gift rules, countering bribery/extortion/money-laundering/terrorist financing, staff awareness-raising, immediate breach notification). Effectiveness indicators: number of reported incidents, frequency of corruption-related events, and the Group's subjective assessment of daily business activity. Controls include due-diligence-based business processes and segregation of duties. Compliance/anti-corruption matters are reported quarterly to the Supervisory Board Audit Committee and semi-annually to the Supervisory Board, and presented to the Management Board President at regular meetings. The Risk Management and Compliance Director analyzes identified corruption threats, assesses risks, prepares risk-treatment plans where needed, and supervises plan execution delegated to business owners.

Payment practices (G1-6): The Group maintains its reputation as a reliable payer via implemented purchase-invoice workflows enabling timely verification, approval, posting, and settlement. By 30 April each year, reporting-obligated Group companies file commercial-transaction payment-term reports for the prior year (obligation applies to tax capital groups, real-estate companies, and CIT payers with annual revenue exceeding EUR 50M equivalent). Standard payment terms follow invoice receipt per contract; shorter 14-day terms are accepted via negotiation, and companies aim not to exceed the statutory 30-day term, without differentiating suppliers by enterprise size. Average invoice-payment time in the noted companies is 20 days; no court proceedings regarding payment delays are currently pending. The share of payments compliant with standard terms (within 30 days) is 98%. Companies obligated to file reports: Dom Development S.A., Dom Construction Sp. z o.o., Dom Development Wrocław Sp. z o.o., Euro Styl S.A., Euro Styl Construction Sp. z o.o.
