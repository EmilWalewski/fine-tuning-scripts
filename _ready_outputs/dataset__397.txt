Excerpt from the Management Report on operations of Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group for 2025 (Grupa Kapitałowa Dom Development S.A.), covering the consolidated sustainability statement under ESRS E4 (biodiversity/ecosystems) and ESRS E5 (resource use and circular economy).

E4-5 (biodiversity and ecosystem change impact metrics): The Group selected voluntary metrics—not legally required—based on applicable national regulation, including land area excluded from agricultural production, mass of soil designated for removal under remediation, number of vegetation plantings, and number of trees designated for felling in areas under nature-protection forms or within 500 m of a project (referenced in the table on pp. 81-83). Metrics relate to vegetation planting, land-use change, and natural-resource exploitation impacts on biodiversity. National regulations and internal procedures require planting of native, habitat-compatible species, benefiting ecosystem diversity. Metrics are collected across all operating locations, derived from administrative decisions issued in the reporting year, and monitored annually. The Group references no specific ecological thresholds, official guidelines, policies, or agreements and has set no measurable biodiversity targets. It does not disclose land use based on life-cycle assessment. The Group conducts no activities causing change in sea use, direct exploitation in nature-valuable areas, or spread of invasive alien species. Biodiversity actions are executed within development-project budgets; costs appear under "external services" (outside services) in note 7.35 of the consolidated financial statements. Land remediation applies when acquiring industrial property or where historic land-surface contamination exists (per the Environmental Protection Law Act).

ESRS E5 (resource use and circular economy):
E5-1 (policies): In 2025 the Group adopted a Sustainable Procurement Standard, an implementing document to the Sustainable Procurement Policy adopted in 2024; approved by the CEOs of all Group companies. The policy is an internal, non-public document, though its principles will be communicated to stakeholders. To move away from primary resources and increase use of secondary resources, in the Green Procurement Standard the Company commits to preferring products and materials with below-standard environmental impact. The Standard sets a goal of optimizing waste management per the waste-handling hierarchy and implementing circular-economy principles; results reported annually. Where feasible and economically justified, the policy spans the full procurement cycle, obliging Group companies to select environmentally and/or socially friendly suppliers, services, and products.

E5-2 (actions and resources): Circular-economy (GOZ) principles are implemented per the ReSOLVE model. To reduce building emissions, the Group plans to gradually increase use of recycled steel (construction rebar). Group companies undertake no collective actions to strengthen the circular economy. Ongoing 2025 actions: raising employee and subcontractor-employee awareness via educational posters on water/energy saving and waste segregation at headquarters and construction-site offices; training on municipal, construction, and demolition waste segregation; minimizing primary-raw-material use through pre-execution purchasing/contracting based on BIM360 platform data and building-structure optimization; digitalization of supplier/subcontractor contracting; employee sales and equipment donations to charity; construction/demolition waste segregation and monitoring. IT-equipment life is extended—computers/laptops used at least 5 years, phones at least 3 years (more in E5-5). Projects use BIM (Building Information Modelling) methodology with CDE (Common Data Environment) platforms; over 1,700 active users in 2025 on the Group's single platform, supporting design-documentation updates, contractor-designer communication, material-documentation approval, and inspector reviews via mobile devices/tablets, reducing printing.

E5-3 (targets): The Sustainable Procurement Standard supports the ESG DOM 2030 strategy, targeting asset-value retention, circular design for greater investment durability, ESG integration in contracting, and lower-impact materials. It introduces supplier surveying and embeds environmental/social criteria in negotiations; progress reported annually in chapter E5-2. Targets are voluntary, set internally after analysis of national and EU law and economic feasibility, without ecological thresholds, scientific evidence, or external stakeholder involvement.
