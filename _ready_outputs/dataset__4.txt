The excerpt frames online investment platforms and e-brokerage as fast-growing digital financial services segments shaped by mobile investing, financial-services digitalization and cloud infrastructure. The online trading platform market was estimated at USD 11.65bn in 2025 and forecast to reach USD 18.18bn by 2031, implying about 7.7% CAGR in 2026-2031; the global e-brokerage market was estimated at USD 6.05bn in 2025 and forecast at USD 8.97bn by 2031, about 6.8% CAGR. Cloud-based solutions represented over 63% of the trading platform market in 2025, supporting broker infrastructure scaling during higher investor activity. Retail investors accounted for about 69.6% of the trading platform market and 62.8% of the e-brokerage market, with activity supported by mobile application development and educational and social tools for individual investors. Despite the spread of commission-free models, transaction fees remained a key broker revenue source, representing about 59.7% of the trading platform market value in 2025; platforms also diversified through interest on client funds, subscriptions and robo-advisory services. North America remained the largest geographic market, generating about 33.9-40.9% of global sector revenue in 2025. Asia-Pacific showed the fastest expected growth, about 9-14% CAGR, supported by smartphone penetration and capital-market liberalization. Europe remained stable but increasingly regulation-driven, including the planned ban on payment for order flow from 2026. Latin America offered material growth potential as smartphone penetration supported mobile investment platforms and new retail investor inflows. XTB Group was positioned among leading international brokerage houses and as a Polish market leader; its competitive environment comprised brokers offering investment products through All-in-One trading platforms and mobile apps, fintech companies and CFD-only brokers. The macro backdrop included record tariff increases in a single year, the highest for decades, with neutral forecasts indicating an average level of about 15-18%; tariff announcements caused short-term equity-index declines, particularly in automotive, electronics and steel, while commodity and energy markets reacted through higher production costs and supply-chain disruptions, translating into higher prices for companies and consumers. Amid rising uncertainty, investors increased exposure to safe-haven assets such as gold and government bonds; in 2025 the gold price per ounce rose from about USD 2,600-3,000 to about USD 4,368 at end-December, a value increase of about 60-70%.
