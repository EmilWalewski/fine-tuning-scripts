The IBOR reform subsection describes the Polish reference-rate transition. Following the close, on 1 July 2024, of opinion-gathering under the public consultation process, the Steering Committee of the National Working Group, at meetings on 21 November 2024 and 6 December 2024, deliberated and decided to select the index proposal with the technical name WIRF- as the target reference interest-rate benchmark intended to replace the WIBOR benchmark. On 24 January 2025 the Steering Committee decided on the target name POLSTR (Polish Short Term Rate) for the index proposal technically named WIRF-. On 28 March 2025 the Steering Committee of the National Working Group for reference-rate reform (KS NGR) approved an updated Roadmap for the process of replacing the WIBOR and WIBID benchmarks. Per the latest NGR update, in 2026 a verification will occur of the conditions for the occurrence of a regulatory event as defined in Article 23c(1) of the BMR Regulation. Occurrence of that regulatory event would initiate the statutory procedure resulting in issuance of a Regulation of the Minister of Finance designating the replacement for the key WIBOR benchmark, together with the adjustment spread and the date from which the replacement will apply; the regulation would enter into force after a vacatio legis period providing time to adapt. The stated objective of Poland's reference-rate reform is a safe and efficient transition to the new type of benchmark from end-December 2027.

To the best of the Group's knowledge, it does not expect a material impact of the IBOR reform on its financial liabilities, but cannot definitively determine the impact because not all systemic and regulatory solutions related to the reform have been developed. The Group is taking actions to ensure readiness to change reference rates in concluded financial instruments should WIBOR publication cease; specifically, it continuously monitors regulatory changes regarding benchmarks and negotiates amendments to the Framework Agreements and credit agreements under which hedging instruments and bank credits granted to Group companies are respectively concluded, to incorporate optimal procedures enabling migration to an alternative benchmark when it replaces WIBOR.
