Consolidated Sustainability Report (Section 3), subsection S1-1 "Policies related to own workforce" within the Management Board Report on the operations of Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group for 2025. Governing documents on employee cooperation across the Group: Human Capital Management Strategy (adopted 2025 as execution of a goal set in the ESG Strategy DOM 2030) and the Employment Policy; supplemented by work, bonus, remuneration, and non-wage benefits regulations. The Strategy is an integral part of the Group's business strategy. Strategy priorities: expanding knowledge and developing employee competencies in key business areas; creating vertical and horizontal development paths to align competencies with organizational needs and job profiles; strengthening a values-based leadership model; developing an early-career talent pool via internship and student apprenticeship programs; assessing employee results and potential; offering attractive, competitive employment terms in both pay and benefits with pay transparency and gender equality; offering integration, social, and well-being activities; building positive work culture and a safe work environment; monitoring and counteracting discrimination and unequal treatment and ensuring gender equality including counteracting the gender pay gap; respecting all employee rights consistent with international human rights regulations and best market practices; absolute prohibition and elimination of human trafficking, forced/compulsory labor, and child labor; workplace accident prevention via internal risk-mitigating regulations; planning and sourcing candidates per organizational needs through external recruitment and an internal referral program; onboarding new employees into firm structures and processes and integration with organizational values.

Employment Policy: defines key human capital management directions and supports Group strategic goals; covers persons employed under employment contracts and those performing tasks under civil-law contracts; specifies obligations of all Group companies regarding employment; aims at legal compliance, defining and promoting healthy work-life balance, and addressing equal opportunity and non-discrimination (by gender, race, religion, nationality, sexual orientation, age, or disability). Key labor-rights elements: equality and non-discrimination, stable forms of employment, social dialogue, work safety, remuneration and benefits. Recruitment/selection criteria: qualifications, skills, experience, motivation, predisposition to the advertised position. Development, training, and improvement are embedded in the Employment Policy via internal/external training, industry conferences, seminars, workshops, and language courses. No special anti-discrimination procedures were implemented in Group companies; violations may be reported under the Whistleblowing and Whistleblower Protection Procedure. The Policy states Group companies oppose all forms of forced labor, human trafficking, and worker exploitation, and observe an absolute child-labor ban. Through human capital management documents, companies commit to: stable employment forms; respecting the right to form/join trade unions; safe and hygienic working conditions; flexible work and work-life balance; facilitating return to work after long leave; social and occupational reintegration programs; equal and non-discriminatory treatment in HR processes; employee development, training, and improvement; promoting ethical values; structured recruitment and selection rules; a transparent remuneration and benefits system; zero tolerance for irregularities; and an appropriate atmosphere and organizational culture respecting workplace diversity.

Workplace accident prevention is addressed by the Group's Occupational Health, Safety, and Environmental Protection Policy, which enables: identification and minimization of injury/health-loss risk for all persons affected by Group operations (employees, customers, members of the public); continuous improvement of environmental, health, and OHS standards across the Group; promotion of employee health, safety, and well-being; ensuring legal obligations of the Group and its employees are met; and achieving full Group compliance with OHS and environmental standards by 2030.

The Group's overall approach to human and labor rights is comprehensively regulated in three key documents: the Fundamental Rights Policy, the Employment Policy, and the Code of Ethical Conduct. The Company aims to ensure human rights compliance and create equal employment opportunities regardless of gender, race, age, religion, or origin, and commits to protecting rights of indigenous peoples, national/ethnic/religious/linguistic minorities, women, children, persons with disabilities, and migrant workers and their families. The Fundamental Rights Policy and Human Capital Management Strategy incorporate the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, the ILO Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work, and the OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises, requiring Group companies to monitor and observe these provisions via: identifying and managing fundamental-rights violation risks including during internal due diligence; mitigating risks within the Policy scope; and monitoring effectiveness. Upon a confirmed breach, the Policy obliges Group companies to undertake remedial action, which may include terminating a contractor agreement, suspending supplies from the contractor, or demanding the contractor take corrective measures. Group company Management Boards are responsible for implementation and oversight of these policies.

Companies ensure employees free access to information on policies, rights, and obligations, and provide broad internal communication (mailing, intranet), communicating weekly on current employment and professional-development matters and promoting a culture of openness and dialogue. The Group conducts satisfaction surveys and questionnaires, collects employee feedback to improve communication processes and methods, and incorporates these opinions. Clear rules and procedures govern equal-treatment and employment practices. In 2025, Group companies conducted no training on non-discrimination policies and practices. Job requirements are not defined in a discriminatory manner; companies avoid unjustified, exclusionary requirements. Per applicable law and internal regulations, companies maintain current documentation on recruitment, training, and promotions reflecting employee capabilities and career development. The above policies account for the interests of key stakeholders.
