In the first half of 2025, Dom Development Group continued to emphasize systematic acquisition of attractive land for new residential projects as a key success factor. The Group pursued land purchases with regard to current market conditions and competitive pricing, supporting satisfactory margins. In a market with limited supply of legally and planning-ready land, especially in attractive urban locations, the ability to secure new investment sites remained an important competitive advantage. As of June 30, 2025, the Group’s land bank covered nearly 19,000 units across its four main markets: Warsaw, Tricity, Wrocław and Kraków. Management considered this level appropriate given local administrative procedures, regulatory volatility affecting project preparation, and housing finance market conditions. The Group’s land resources allow it to replenish its offer flexibly, respond to market demand and maintain a leading position. Its acquisition efforts are supported by experienced teams and strong liquidity, enabling quick execution of significant land transactions.

In sustainability risk management, the Group identified threats related to climate change, social issues and governance through a double materiality analysis. These were divided into physical and transition risks, along with a range of opportunities. ESG risks and opportunities are relevant to the Group’s strategy and financial planning, and the identified risks were entered into the risk register as part of its risk management policy. The Group also addressed mitigation measures. Based on national climate change forecasts, it assessed physical and transition climate risks in specific operating locations through 2050 and their potential impact on assets. Warsaw, Kraków and Wrocław are expected to face more days with extremely high temperatures, with the strongest change in Wrocław, where by 2050 there may be six more such days than in the current decade. These changes may lengthen dry periods with high temperatures and intensify the urban heat island effect. Another physical risk is higher average annual precipitation, expected to be most noticeable in Kraków, as well as more days with rainfall above 20 mm. This may increase the probability of river floods in Warsaw, Kraków and Wrocław, sea-related flooding in Tricity, and urban flash floods in Tricity.
