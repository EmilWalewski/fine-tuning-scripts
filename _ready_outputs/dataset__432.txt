Excerpt from Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group's 2025 Management Report, Section 3 Consolidated Sustainability Reporting, disclosure SBM-3 (Material Impacts, Risks and Opportunities and their Interaction with Strategy and Business Model). Content is qualitative ESG/double-materiality narrative with no financial figures.

Workers in the value chain: Group exerts indirect influence over value-chain workers via mandatory Code of Conduct compliance and established working-condition rules; fair-competition practices positively affect wages of personnel on Group construction sites. Group affects occupational health and safety (BHP/OHS) on construction sites where work is performed by personnel employed by Subcontractors; the construction sector is broadly regarded as dangerous and accident-prone. Forced labour and other labour-related rights deemed material in the value chain: Group companies do not use raw materials extracted (or products manufactured) in countries at risk of forced labour, and materials used in the realization process originate predominantly from within the EU. Reference to EU Regulation 2024/3015 prohibiting products made with forced labour on the EU market, and to amendment of Directive (EU) 2019/1937, amid rising sustainable value-chain requirements.

Affected communities / sustainable cities (entity-specific disclosure): as investor and general contractor, Group plays an important role in transforming cities and regions where it operates, supporting development of these areas, strengthening their cohesion, increasing attractiveness, and contributing to revival of local economic and social environments. Group companies influence relations with surroundings and local communities through actions defined in their CSR Strategies; conversely, project realization and associated nuisances may negatively affect local communities. Identified risks/impacts: limited ability to react quickly to market changes and develop new projects, affecting cash flows; potential increased costs from adapting projects to local community expectations; long-term loss of trust limiting ability to attract investment and develop operations, affecting financial results; waiting on administrative decisions may impair revenue generation; increased costs from negotiations/mediation; loss of investor and client trust potentially lowering revenues; delays in obtaining permits may delay project completion and revenue generation; investment delays generating additional costs and reduced cash flows (e.g., project suspension or modification); risk of dependence of investment projects on municipality-shaping local-government decisions plus accompanying infrastructure; failure to deliver on commitments made in strategic and local pro-social documents with associated reputational risk; rising number of local-community protests linked to nuisances from investments. Opportunities: greater financial stability through improved relations with investors, in turn easing access to capital; brand and corporate image building.

Consumers and end-users / social inclusion of consumers (responsible practices): Group companies affect information conveyed to external stakeholders — including individual clients, end-users, and also investors and shareholders, in part via marketing — through socially responsible practices fair toward clients; information is verified and subject to internal control. Related risks/impacts: potential reduction in company value from reputational risk and lower credibility in compliance and governance; costs of renegotiating contracts or seeking new Contractors affecting cash flows; potential administrative penalty costs; costs of developing and implementing control processes and training staff responsible for communication; increased cost of corrective actions; reduction in number of contracts and projects with business partners lowering cash flows. Opportunity: avoidance of costs from potential legal disputes arising from transmission of erroneous information.

Whistleblower protection (Ochrona sygnalistów): misconduct-reporting channels operating within Group companies are not sufficiently made available and communicated. Associated risks: risk of non-reporting of abuses; reputational risk; risk of loss of employee trust; potential conflicts or loss of business partners.

Supplier relationship management / payment practices (Postępowanie w biznesie): process of negotiating and setting contractual payment terms with Contractors/suppliers in the Group each time accounts for provisions of the Act of 4 November 2022 amending the Act on counteracting excessive delays in commercial transactions and the Act on public finances, aimed at protecting smaller enterprises in asymmetric relations with a debtor that is a large entrepreneur.
