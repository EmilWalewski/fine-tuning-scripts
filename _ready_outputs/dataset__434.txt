Narrative: frequent changes in Polish VAT, CIT, and social-security regulations create interpretive uncertainty, scarce precedents, and elevated Polish tax risk versus more mature systems; tax settlements (including customs and FX matters) subject to authority inspection with high penalties and interest; despite a very prudent tax policy, amounts disclosed could change following final tax-authority decisions, though deemed unlikely. General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) introduced into the Tax Ordinance effective 15 July 2016 targets artificial legal structures created primarily to obtain tax benefits contrary to the purpose of tax law; indicators include unjustified splitting of operations, engaging intermediary entities lacking economic justification, mutually offsetting/compensating elements, and similar actions; GAAR applies to transactions after its entry into force and to pre-existing transactions yielding ongoing benefits thereafter, enabling authorities to challenge group restructuring and reorganization arrangements; requires significantly greater judgment in assessing transaction tax effects.
