Excerpt from Dom Development S.A. Capital Group's 2025 Management Report, Section 3 (Consolidated Sustainability Reporting), covering employment, environment, anti-corruption, competition, and consumer interests. Employment and labor relations governed by the Human Capital Management Strategy, an internal framework integral to overall business strategy, defining HR mission and priorities centered on being employer of first choice, building long-term trust-based relationships, and an engaged work environment; key strategic areas include competency management, attractive employment terms, top-candidate recruitment, and an HR model supporting operations and internal communication; aligned with international human rights and labor guidelines, including the ILO Declaration and OECD guidelines. The Employment Policy supplements the Strategy, framing employment rules, professional development, work-life balance, vocational and social reintegration, equality, flexibility, and dialogue; Personnel defined as persons under employment contracts and civil-law contracts. Occupational health and safety governed by the Occupational Health, Safety and Environmental Protection Policy, aimed at continuous standard improvement and minimizing injury/health risks to employees, clients, and the public; construction sites undergo OHS reviews at least weekly; employees participate in safety and first-aid training. In 2025 the Group developed climate alerts to protect employee health and safety and minimize risks from extreme weather, distributed by the Non-Financial Reporting Team upon IMGW or RCB alerts for a given region. Environmental matters governed primarily by the ESG DOM2030 Strategy, setting Group targets through 2030. The Occupational Health, Safety and Environmental Protection Policy targets identification and minimization of environmental hazards, addressing pollution prevention, waste reduction, efficient use of materials, energy and water, and use of local/natural materials with lower carbon footprint; due diligence includes activity reviews to identify significant environmental impacts, risk management procedures, crisis response capability, supply-chain engagement, employee awareness and training at all levels, and a waste management plan for every project; policy subject to regular review. The Group's Sustainable Procurement Policy aims at responsible supply-chain management supporting ESG DOM 2030, communicating supplier expectations, effective partnership cooperation, incorporating environmental, social and governance (ESG) criteria into purchasing decisions, and balancing business profitability with broader social interest. In 2025, under the ESG DOM 2030 Strategy, the Group implemented a Sustainable Procurement Standard defining purchasing policy execution rules; key objectives: reducing the Company's negative environmental impact by promoting lower-impact products/services; increasing cost efficiency of material and service procurement, lowering operating costs, and applying closed-loop (circular) design principles to increase investment durability; and supporting Contractors in implementing ESG principles. Environmental due diligence is enabled by the Environmental Emergency Plan, a Group implementation standard identifying potential threats, applicable to all construction sites and activities of the Group and its Subcontractors; implementation rests with the ESG Coordinator, with annual verification by the OHS Director and ESG Coordinator. Anti-bribery and anti-corruption addressed via the Anti-Corruption, Gift and Conflict of Interest Policy, expressing total rejection of corruption; main objectives: minimizing corruption risk among parties engaged in business cooperation, managing non-compliance risk as a due-diligence instrument, requiring absolute integrity within legal bounds from all engaged parties, and preventing conflicts of interest; the Policy regulates anti-corruption rules for Personnel and business Partners, gift acceptance/giving rules to minimize corruption risk, corruption-threat identification, competition protection, and conflict-of-interest management. Competition matters covered by the Good Practices Policy on Stakeholder Relations, establishing open, transparent dialogue with Contractors/business partners, shareholders, local communities, clients and other stakeholders; commitments include using varied communication channels, regularly gathering and timely, reliable, transparent responses to feedback, and providing only true and complete information; an integral part of organizational culture and a non-compliance risk management instrument; implementation and updating by the Risk Management and Compliance Director, with oversight by the Company's Management Board. Consumer interests: the Good Practices Policy on Stakeholder Relations contains client-service commitments safeguarding consumer interests, particularly residential-unit purchasers, covering legal compliance and financial protection of buyers, offer/communication transparency and reliability, service process and risk management, handover and after-sales service, and complaints and dispute resolution; the Group also commits to measuring and monitoring customer satisfaction and ensuring convenient service conditions, including individual solutions for clients in difficult life situations.
