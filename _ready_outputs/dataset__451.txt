Dom Development S.A. Capital Group H1 2025 Management Report, Section 2 (Operations Summary), covering inflation, capital availability, labor market, and GDP growth. Inflation: core inflation (excluding food and energy) reached 3.4% y/y in June 2025, a slight rise from 3.3% in May, ending a downtrend lasting over a year; headline CPI stood at 4.1% y/y, up from 4.0% in May, with a monthly change of 0.1%, signaling continued stabilization of consumer price growth. Per the National Bank of Poland's July 2025 inflation projection, CPI is forecast in the 3.9–6.6% range for 2025 and 1.3–4.1% for 2026, with core inflation expected to remain at the upper bounds of the inflation target across the projection horizon. Real estate remains among the most frequently chosen forms of capital protection against inflation; within the Group, the share of cash transactions in H1 2025 sales was 47%, a slight decline versus 55% in the prior-year comparable period. High inflation, a still-strong rental market, and investment uncertainty in other segments support sustained interest in property as an investment. On the cost side, inflation had a moderate impact on Group operations. In June 2025 the domestic construction materials market continued stabilizing: average annual price dynamics were –0.9%, the weakest decline in 10 months, with month-over-month prices virtually unchanged, suggesting an approaching end to the price-decline phase. Subcontractor activity remains affected by relatively high fuel and energy prices and persistent wage pressure. The Group uses its own general contractors, optimizing the construction process from the planning phase, enabling economies of scale, stable cooperation with trusted subcontractors, and long-term partnership relations—a significant competitive advantage. Management assesses this operating model substantially limits the impact of rising execution costs and secures appropriate profitability.

Capital Availability: Management prioritizes maintaining high liquidity at both the standalone and Group levels. Management assesses the Group has limited exposure to capital-shortage risk, supported by low net debt, high available cash, and consistently high creditworthiness ratings from financial institutions, providing competitive advantage and security for investment plans. As at 30 June 2025, the Group held cash of PLN 870M (including PLN 134M held in residential escrow/trust accounts) and undrawn credit lines totaling PLN 715M. Interest-bearing debt stood at PLN 799M, of which only PLN 209M was short-term debt. As a result, the Group maintained a positive net cash balance of PLN 71M.

Labor Market: Housing market condition remains strongly correlated with the labor market; Poland remains among EU states with the lowest unemployment, supporting demand stability for residential real estate. Management views the H1 2025 labor market as meaningful support for stable housing demand, particularly in large agglomerations where the Group operates. Per Central Statistical Office (GUS) data, registered unemployment in June 2025 was 5.1%, remaining relatively low and stable. Labor markets in Warsaw, the Tri-City (Trójmiasto), Wrocław, and Kraków—the Group's principal areas of operation—showed even lower unemployment, below the so-called natural rate. Growth in average gross wages in the enterprise sector remained high, with annual dynamics of 11.7% in H1 2025; despite a smoothing trend in wage growth, real wage levels—supported by declining inflationary pressure—increased consumer purchasing power, positively affecting household propensity to purchase apartments among both individual and investment clients. Employment stability affecting creditworthiness and the purchasing decisions of externally financed clients remains material; consumer expectations regarding employment durability and income growth are key to continuing the positive apartment-sales trend. Management considers high professional activity and employment stability in the largest urban centers, combined with observed wage growth, to be among the key factors underpinning housing-market demand fundamentals, with the Group's concentration on the most developed local markets allowing full benefit from these favorable macroeconomic conditions.

GDP Growth: The housing market is a cyclical economic segment, with the developer sector substantially tied to GDP growth. In H1 2025 Poland continued stable economic growth—Q1 real GDP rose 3.2% y/y (0.7% q/q), confirming economic resilience. Full-year macroeconomic forecasts remain optimistic: the European Commission assumes 3.3% GDP growth in 2025, while other institutions (EBRD and CBRE) forecast dynamics of approximately 3.3–3.4%. Growth is supported chiefly by strong private and public consumption, favorable labor-market trends, rising real wages, and easing inflationary pressure. Although early 2025 brought falling inflation and interest-rate cuts, regulatory changes and global economic factors remain risks. A potential economic slowdown in Poland could affect Group operations, especially in the largest agglomerations where its development activity dominates; however, Management assesses that Dom Development S.A. and its Capital Group, owing to a strong financial position and well-developed organizational structure, show relatively high resilience to cyclical fluctuations.
