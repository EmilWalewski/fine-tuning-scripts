The section describes XTB Group’s liquidity risk as the risk of losing the ability to finance assets and meet liabilities on time in normal or foreseeable conditions without incurring a loss. Liquidity management considers current access to liquid funds, future needs, alternative scenarios and contingency plans. The parent company manages liquidity by maintaining sufficient cash on appropriate bank accounts to cover required operations. For selected accounts connected with financial instrument activity, it defines a safe range of free cash: excess above the upper limit is transferred to a current account, while cash is replenished when balances fall to the lower limit.

Subsidiaries manage liquidity through expected cash flow analysis and by matching asset maturities with liability maturities. They do not use models, but gap-based liquidity management is considered effective and sufficient. No liquidity shortages or inability to meet financial obligations occurred in subsidiaries. In extraordinary cases, the parent company may provide liquidity support. Deviations from the liquidity procedure require approval by at least two members of the parent company’s Management Board and notification to the Risk Control Department.

The parent company has contingency liquidity plans, but they were not used in the reporting or comparative period because the most liquid assets, mainly own cash and Treasury bonds or bonds guaranteed by the State Treasury, significantly exceeded liabilities and future liquidity needs. Cash on accounts is monitored continuously by relevant organizational unit managers, while the Risk Control Department performs daily oversight and control of cash accounts. Under the ICARAP process, the parent company identifies liquidity and funding risk factors and assesses whether liquid assets are adequate to cover current, future and stressed liquidity needs. Under IFR rules effective from 26 September 2021, the parent company maintains liquid assets equal to at least one third of its fixed overhead requirement. As of the report date, the parent company’s liquid assets were 12 times higher than the IFR requirement.
