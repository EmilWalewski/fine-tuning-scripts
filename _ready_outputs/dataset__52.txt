Dom Development S.A. and its group face competitive risk mainly in acquiring suitable land, where other developers may bid for the same plots and drive land prices higher. Competition may also lead to an oversupply of apartments if too many development projects are available on the market. Either effect could materially harm the company’s operations, financial position or results. At the same time, the group reported very strong sales momentum: the fourth quarter of 2025 was its best quarter to date, with 1,232 units sold, and the sixth consecutive quarter with at least 1,000 units sold. For the full year, the group sold a record 4,448 units. The attractiveness and diversity of its offer supported demand from both mortgage-financed buyers and cash buyers.

The legal risk environment changed significantly in 2025 across spatial planning, the investment and construction process, customer relations and housing communities. These changes are described as orderly, digital and restrictive, but they may increase investment costs, reduce project flexibility and raise regulatory risk for developers.

A key planning change is the extension of existing municipal spatial development studies until 30 June 2026. This reduced the risk of a planning gap and helped preserve continuity in zoning decisions and local plan adoption. However, the planned introduction of general municipal plans as binding spatial policy tools may create new risks: changes to permitted development of land already held or being acquired, lower plot capacity, longer administrative procedures and possible temporary suspension of zoning decisions in municipalities that fail to adopt general plans on time.

The housing special act was maintained in 2025 but is set to expire on 1 July 2026, with already initiated proceedings allowed to continue. Its phase-out reduces flexibility in carrying out projects, especially in cities, and forces new projects to rely more heavily on traditional planning instruments. This may require redesigns or reduce the number of projects that can be implemented.

Amendments to the developer act require full transparency of prices for apartments and related products, including publication of current prices, price-change history and reporting to public systems. This improves market transparency but limits pricing and negotiation flexibility and increases the risk of sanctions if price information is inconsistent across sales channels. A further amendment entering into force in the first quarter of 2026 strengthens information duties on how the usable floor area forming the basis of the price is calculated, requiring consistency between prospectuses, contracts and technical documentation. Rules adopted in 2025 are also intended to create the Portal DOM in the second quarter of 2027, a government system covering housing transactions, prices, price changes and sales status. This will function as a control tool and will require the company to report current and consistent data.

Technical and construction changes, including those related to EU rules on building energy performance, introduce zero-emission standards, broader building automation requirements and stricter fire safety rules. These may increase construction costs, limit development parameters, require redesigns or more expensive technical solutions, and affect margins and apartment prices. From 2026, civil protection and civil defence rules require underground floors and garages to be designed as temporary shelter areas, increasing design complexity and costs. The lack of uniform interpretation adds regulatory uncertainty.

From January 2026, amendments to construction law require appeals and complaints to include specific allegations, the scope of the request and supporting evidence, with formal defects resulting in the submission being left unexamined. For the company, this may make it easier to eliminate vague appeals filed only to delay proceedings. A draft amendment to the ownership of premises act from January 2026 is intended to clarify housing community operations and separation of premises, including limits on separating units in collective residence buildings, treating structural balcony elements as common property and strengthening communities’ ability to pursue claims for defects in common areas. This may increase developers’ post-sale liability and affect mixed-use, PRS and aparthotel projects. From 1 January 2026, e-Deliveries became the main communication channel with public administration, while the implementation of KSeF and JPK_CIT increases digitisation and tax settlement control.
