The Group recognizes contract revenue using the stage-of-completion method. When estimated revenue exceeds amounts invoiced, the difference is recognized in service revenue and as other current assets. When estimated revenue is lower than invoiced revenue, the difference is recognized in service revenue and as deferred income. Revenue from non-construction services, including estate administration services, is recognized in the period in which the service is provided.

Current tax receivables and liabilities are measured at amounts expected to be paid to or recovered from tax authorities, using tax rates and regulations in force at the reporting date. Deferred tax is calculated using the balance-sheet liability method on temporary differences between tax bases and carrying amounts of assets and liabilities. Deferred tax assets are recognized for deductible temporary differences, unused tax credits and tax losses carried forward to the extent that taxable income is probable. Their carrying amount is reviewed at each reporting date and reduced if sufficient taxable income is no longer probable; previously unrecognized deferred tax assets are reassessed and recognized when future taxable profits are probable. Deferred tax liabilities are recognized for taxable temporary differences expected to increase the future tax base. Deferred tax assets and liabilities are measured using tax rates expected to apply when the asset is realized or liability settled, based on rates and laws enacted or substantively enacted at the reporting date. Income tax relating to items outside profit or loss is also recognized outside profit or loss, in other comprehensive income or directly in equity as appropriate. Deferred tax assets and liabilities are offset only when the Group has an enforceable legal right to offset current tax amounts and the deferred tax relates to the same taxpayer and tax authority.

Dividends are recognized when shareholders’ rights to receive them are established. Net earnings per share are calculated by dividing net profit for the period by the weighted average number of shares in the reporting period.

For leases longer than 12 months, except low-value assets, the Group recognizes right-of-use assets and lease liabilities and presents depreciation separately from interest expense. Right-of-use assets are presented in the balance sheet line where the underlying asset would be shown if owned. Rights to use office space and serviced apartments are depreciated, lease finance costs are recognized, the related asset is included in property, plant and equipment, and liabilities are classified as long- or short-term as appropriate.

Lease costs related to perpetual usufruct rights over land used in development projects are capitalized in inventories, specifically work in progress, during project execution. The related asset is presented in inventories or short-term receivables, while the liability is presented wholly as current. This reflects that the land rights relate to properties on which the Group develops projects; the costs are transferred from inventories to profit or loss together with the cost of sold finished goods when units are handed over to customers and revenue is recognized. Land covered by the Act of 20 July 2018 converting perpetual usufruct rights for residential land into ownership is treated analogously, with transformation fees accounted for like perpetual usufruct fees.

Perpetual usufruct rights for development land are generally classified as inventories, and related liabilities are current because they are expected to be settled through transfer to apartment buyers within the Group’s operating cycle. The Group remains legally liable for perpetual usufruct or transformation fees until the notarial transfer of the buyer’s share in the land attached to the sold unit. Until that transfer, the lease liability remains on the Group’s balance sheet. When a unit is handed over, which is also the revenue recognition point, the related portion of the lease asset is transferred from inventories to receivables from the buyer in an amount corresponding to the related lease liability. Both receivable and liability remain current until ownership is transferred, when both are derecognized. Future payments arising from perpetual usufruct rights, which may extend up to 99 years depending on the property, are discounted; this period is based on the legal term of the right rather than the Group’s expected holding period for development purposes.

Management’s professional judgment and assumptions are significant in applying accounting policies, especially for development project budgets. Purchase decisions for land are based on analyses including acquisition budgets used to assess expected project profitability. After acquisition, construction project budgets are updated using management’s best knowledge and experience, reviewed and updated at least every three months. Updated budgets support profitability reviews, assessment of possible inventory write-downs, and preparation of financial forecasts, annual budgets and medium-term plans.
