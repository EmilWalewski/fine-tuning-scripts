The Q3 2025 XTB S.A. Capital Group risk-management section states that the Group is exposed to multiple risks from current operations and manages them to ensure conscious, controlled risk-taking. Risk policies identify and measure accepted risks and set regular limits restricting exposure. At strategic level, the Management Board sets and monitors risk-management policy; all risk types are monitored and controlled against business profitability and the capital level required for operational safety under capital requirements. In the Parent Entity, a Risk Management Committee composed of Supervisory Board members prepares the risk appetite document, reviews risk-management strategy, supports Supervisory Board oversight of Management Board execution of that strategy, and verifies remuneration policy and implementation rules for alignment with the brokerage house risk profile, capital, liquidity, and probability and timing of income generation. The Risk Control Department supports the Management Board in shaping, reviewing and updating ICARAP rules when new risk types or significant strategy and action-plan changes arise; monitors adequacy and effectiveness of the implemented risk-management system; identifies, monitors and controls risks of the Group own investments; calculates capital requirements; and estimates internal capital. The Supervisory Board of the Parent Entity approves the XTB risk-management system. Fair value is defined as the price received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date. The fair value of cash is estimated as close to carrying amount; fair value of financial assets measured at fair value through profit or loss, liabilities to clients and other liabilities is also estimated as close to carrying amount because of short maturities. The fair-value hierarchy uses Level 1 quoted unadjusted active-market prices, Level 2 observable inputs other than Level 1 quoted prices, including prices from less active markets or other valuation techniques using market-observable inputs, and Level 3 unobservable inputs. At 31.12.2024 financial assets - total assets were Level 1 592 116, Level 2 531 807, Level 3 -, total 1 123 923; financial liabilities measured at fair value through profit or loss were Level 2 208 193, total 208 193; financial liabilities - liabilities total were Level 2 208 193, total 208 193. Versus 31.12.2024, total financial assets were lower at 30.09.2025, with lower Level 1 and higher Level 2 balances, while fair-value financial liabilities were higher. No transfers between fair-value hierarchy levels occurred in the periods covered by the interim condensed consolidated financial statements. Fair value of contracts for difference is determined from market prices of underlying instruments from independent sources, including verified liquidity providers and reputable information services, adjusted by the Group spread; closing prices or latest bid and ask prices are used. CFDs are valued as the difference between current price and opening price, including accrued commissions and swap points. The Group estimated that counterparty credit-risk adjustments were not significant for the overall valuation of derivative transactions and therefore, under MSSF 13.73, does not classify those derivative transactions in Level 3. In market risk, during the reporting period the Group entered into OTC contracts for difference and acquired securities, and may also enter into futures on own account on regulated exchange markets. Identified market-risk types are currency risk from exchange-rate movements, interest-rate risk, commodity-price risk and equity-instrument price risk. The primary market-risk management objective is limiting the negative effect of market risk on business profitability. Internal procedures apply limits on market risk from open positions in financial instruments, especially maximum open position per instrument, currency-exposure limits and single-order value limits. The Trading Department continuously monitors open positions constrained by limits and, when limits are exceeded, executes appropriate hedging transactions; the Risk Control Department regularly checks limit utilization and the justification for any hedging transactions. Currency risk arises because the Group mainly enters transactions in instruments exposed to currency risk; beyond transactions with exchange rates as underlying instruments, the Group offers instruments priced in foreign currencies and holds foreign-currency assets, referred to as currency positions. Currency positions comprise the brokerage house own funds denominated in foreign currencies for settlement of transactions on foreign markets and for foreign branches. The report states that carrying amounts of Group assets and liabilities in foreign currencies at the reporting date are presented by base currency in thousands of PLN.
