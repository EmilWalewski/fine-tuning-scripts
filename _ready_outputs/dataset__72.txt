Dom Development S.A. Group notes land acquisition risk as a developer challenge: insufficient suitable plots for multifamily housing could make attractive land harder to obtain, weaken offer volume or structure, and deteriorate sales and financial results. The Management Board emphasizes securing attractive land for new projects; the Group holds a diversified land bank on every operating market, supported by employee experience and cash enabling quick completion of large transactions. Risks are identified early in land purchases and matched with mitigation measures. The Group maintains scale and offer replenishment through a high-quality, appropriately sized land bank enabling sales for about 4 years, considered adequate for local market conditions, long permitting, legislative changes in project preparation and credit-market conditions. Land resources support offer replenishment, market adaptation and the Group position as leader of the Polish residential market; acquisitions follow an internally developed methodology. Project preparation and execution require major outlays before and during construction, while positive cash flows often arise only 15-18 months after construction starts. Key risks include failure to obtain permits for planned land use, construction delays, budget overruns, weather changes, subcontractor insolvency or poor service, subcontractor labor disputes, shortages of materials or equipment, accidents, unforeseen technical difficulties, inability to obtain occupancy decisions or other permits, poor coordination, guideline changes, delays or difficulties in design documentation, use of outdated documentation, loss of formal-legal documentation and land-use regulatory changes. These events could delay completion, increase costs, cause revenue loss, block funds invested in land, or prevent completion, materially harming operations and financial position. Own general contractors let the Company and Group optimize construction from planning, use scale effects, provide stable cooperation for proven subcontractors, build trust-based relations, support timeliness and final-product quality, and mitigate unexpected construction-cost increases to sustain satisfactory profitability. Sales and stakeholder-service risk covers pricing, customer contracts and service processes. The Group mainly sells apartments and may face disputes or court proceedings over completed projects, including obligations under construction-work warranties granted to customers; adverse rulings could materially affect operations, financial position or results. The Management Board mitigates defects reducing apartment value or utility through high technical and finishing standards and prompt defect removal. The Group uses stakeholder-service good-practice rules, contractual provisions, monitoring of the abusive-clauses register, ongoing monitoring of court rulings and customer satisfaction indicators. Joint venture risk: BYSEWSKA Sp. z o.o. and DOKI V Sp. z o.o. are classified as joint arrangements whose agreements give all co-investors joint control; significant decisions require unanimous consent, and the separate legal form gives parties no rights to assets or duty to repay liabilities. BYSEWSKA Sp. z o.o.: on 2 July 2024 Euro Styl S.A. signed an investment agreement with Activa JJ Sp. z o.o. to develop several dozen single-family and several dozen multifamily residential buildings with necessary infrastructure; scope covers design, financing, construction and sale of about 550 residential units. Bysewska Sp. z o.o. was created with share capital of 0.1M PLN; as at 31 December 2025 each co-owner held 50%. DOKI V Sp. z o.o.: on 12 July 2024 Euro Styl S.A. signed an investment agreement with Grupa Torus Sp. z o.o. S.K.A., represented by Gdansk-registered Torus Sp. z o.o., for multifamily residential buildings with necessary infrastructure and, if applicable, an accompanying investment; scope covers design, financing, construction and sale of about 550 residential and service units. DOKI V Sp. z o.o. was created with share capital of 0.1M PLN; as at 31 December 2025 each co-owner held 50%. Joint ventures create legal, financial and reputational risks, including partner selection, actual joint control, longer decision-making, reduced flexibility toward market signals, lower synergy, reduced competitiveness and higher project costs. Despite experienced partners and prior profitability analyses, results may differ from assumptions and negatively affect the Company and Group operations and financial position.
