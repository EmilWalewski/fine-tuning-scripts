On 28 September 2024, the Minister of Finance Regulation of 20 September 2024 on submission of information to the Polish Financial Supervision Authority by investment firms, state banks conducting brokerage activities, banks referred to in Art. 70(2) of the Act on Trading in Financial Instruments and custodian banks entered into force. The regulation makes reporting more detailed and comprehensive, aligned with current EU standards, and introduces new definitions, expanded risk-management requirements, stricter reporting rules and DORA digital operational resilience compliance to improve security and financial stability of the investment market. A 19 April 2024 draft amendment to the Accounting Act, Act on Statutory Auditors, Audit Firms and Public Oversight and certain other acts was published by the Government Legislation Centre; its purpose is implementation into national law of Directive (EU) 2022/2464 of 14 December 2022 on corporate sustainability reporting and Commission Delegated Directive (EU) 2023/2775 of 17 October 2023 amending Directive 2013/34/EU. The text records legislative work on the 26 September 2025 Crypto-Assets Market Act: a government bill reached the Sejm on 26 June 2025 to implement Regulation 2023/1114, introduce new solutions for the crypto-assets market sector, strengthen effective supervision and investor protection, support long-term market development, security and expanded supervisory competences; the act was adopted on 26 September 2025 and sent to the President of Poland and the Senate, the Senate considered it on 16 October 2025, submitted 81 amendments and sent it to the Public Finance Committee, which accepted part of the amendments on 6 November 2025 and returned the bill to the Sejm; on 7 November 2025 the Sejm adopted the act and sent it to the President, who vetoed it on 1 December 2025, with further work on implementing MiCA into Polish law continuing. At the beginning of December 2025, a 2 December 2025 draft act on personal investment accounts was published, regulating accumulation of assets by individuals on OKI personal investment accounts and taxation of OKI assets with an asset-value tax; eligible OKI assets include cash in a bank account, cash in a cash account at a brokerage entity, Treasury savings bonds under Art. 101 of the 27 August 2009 Public Finance Act (Journal of Laws 2025, item 1483), insurance capital fund units, investment fund units, voluntary pension fund settlement units, securities traded on regulated markets or alternative trading systems, and non-security financial instruments traded on regulated markets or alternative trading systems; only one investor may accumulate assets on an OKI and only an adult may conclude an OKI agreement. Accessibility transition provisions state that contracts concluded before entry into force may remain unchanged until expiry, but no longer than 28 June 2030, and service providers may until 28 June 2030 offer or provide services using products that do not meet accessibility requirements if they used them for the same type of services before the act entered into force. The Act of 17 October 2025 on handling complaints by financial market entities, the Financial Ombudsman and the Financial Education Fund originated as a government bill submitted to the Sejm on 2 July 2025; it requires all financial market entities to accept complaints electronically, improving client access, communication and complaint handling, makes an electronic response the default where a complaint is submitted electronically without an additional client request, while preserving the client right to a written response on request; the Sejm adopted the act on 12 September 2025, accepted Senate amendments on 17 October 2025, sent it to the President of Poland, and the act entered into force on 13 February 2026. In XTB Group brokerage and fintech activity, financial capital is presented as the foundation of operational stability and client security; own funds and the capital ratio, defined as equity relative to risk-weighted assets and other regulatory obligations, indicate capacity to absorb potential losses and maintain solvency in normal and stressed conditions. XTB regularly monitors the capital ratio and maintains it at a level considered safe for the organization and clients; it is assessed under the BION process conducted by the Polish Financial Supervision Authority. Consolidated operating revenue increased to 2,146.1M PLN in 2025 from 1,873.4M PLN in 2024, while consolidated net profit decreased to 644.2M PLN from 856.9M PLN. Equity was broadly stable at 2,000.5M PLN in 2025 versus 2,003.6M PLN in 2024. XTB Group own cash rose to 1,994.0M PLN from 1,619.5M PLN, while the Group total capital ratio under IFR fell to 186.0% from 192.3%. Human, intellectual and organizational capital are described as the basis of competitive advantage and growth capacity: human capital covers experience, competences and skills of operational, technology and analytical teams needed for client service, risk management and innovative product implementation; intellectual capital covers organizational know-how, proprietary technologies, trading algorithms, risk-management procedures and analytical systems supporting process efficiency and development of the XTB investment application and platform; organizational capital covers internal structure, decision-making processes, corporate culture and cross-team cooperation mechanisms enabling effective use of human and intellectual resources.
