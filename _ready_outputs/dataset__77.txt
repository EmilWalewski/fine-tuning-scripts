The Group reports greenhouse gas emissions for the entire Capital Group, with 2021 as the base year. Scope 1 emissions are calculated under The GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard; emission factors for natural gas, LPG and diesel from gasoline use DEFRA data, while gasoline and heating oil factors use data published by KOBiZE, with calorific values for gasoline and diesel converted from PKN Orlen fuel safety data sheets. The applied factors were published by credible institutions and are applicable to the entire Group. Scope 2 emissions are calculated under The GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard. Location-based electricity intensity for Poland is 524.94 kgCO2/MWh based on KOBiZE data. Market-based Scope 2 emissions use data from electricity suppliers EON Polska S.A., Polenergia Sp. z o.o., Tauron S.A. and ENERGA S.A. and issued energy-origin guarantees; these guarantees reduced market-based emissions by 55%. In 2025 Group companies purchased 12,385 MWh of renewable energy under signed contracts, assuming 100% of contracted guarantees will be redeemed by Group companies. Heat-energy emission factors use 2024 URE data. Calculations apply IPCC-published 100-year global warming potentials to calculate CO2-equivalent emissions for non-CO2 gases. Reported consumption is based on physical meter readings or booked invoices. No greenhouse gas emissions from joint ventures required notification or consolidation because they are not related entities. The Group has no separate policy for water-resource management, including sustainable development of oceans and seas; a separate policy is planned no earlier than 2030. To determine whether Group companies operate in areas with high water scarcity, the Group used the publicly available free Aqueduct Water Risk Atlas. None of its investments is located in an area of high or very high water stress. Group companies do not purchase water from areas under high or very high water stress and do not operate in such areas. Water is used primarily for construction works, including binding of mixtures during concreting, masonry and plastering and summer concrete curing; investments also use water for sanitary, household and fire-protection purposes. Water is also used in demolition works to prevent excessive dust. Purchased water comes mainly from municipal water-supply networks in cities where Group companies operate. The companies do not abstract water from surface waters, including marine waters. Sanitary sewage systems discharge wastewater to municipal sewerage systems and then to municipal wastewater treatment plants; the companies do not discharge wastewater directly to the environment. Under applicable law, rainwater is managed within investments or discharged to municipal sewerage systems. On-site rainwater management depends on local ground and water conditions and may use technical solutions, including retention tanks, pipe tanks or infiltration boxes enabling gradual rainwater outflow and soil distribution, or natural solutions, including rain gardens supporting biodiversity and/or green roofs enabling rainwater retention. Wastewater from garage floors undergoes additional treatment in petroleum-substance separators. Changes in Group water consumption depend on project execution phases. Water-resource management measures are ongoing activities linked to development projects, so the Group has not adopted specific time horizons and has not developed an action plan requiring significant operating expenditure or capital expenditure. Because it does not operate in water-risk areas, including areas with significant water scarcity, the Group does not implement actions in this area and does not conduct collective actions for water and marine resources. The Group has not set measurable, outcome-oriented water-management targets, consciously deciding so because concrete curing, a water-intensive process requiring proper cement hydration, is one of the key investment-execution processes; without concrete curing, project execution and therefore the Group’s business activity would not be possible. The Group will set water-management targets no earlier than 2030.
