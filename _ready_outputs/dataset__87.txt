XTB S.A. 2025 consolidated annual report excerpt describes statutory bodies and General Meeting rules. The operating procedure and powers of the General Meeting are set out in the Company Statute and the Rules of the General Meeting of XTB S.A. with its registered office in Warsaw, available at ir.xtb.com in the Corporate Governance section. The General Meeting is convened by the Management Board as ordinary or extraordinary. The Annual General Meeting is held every year within six months after the end of the financial year; the financial year equals the calendar year, so the meeting should take place by the end of June. An Extraordinary General Meeting is convened in cases specified in the Commercial Companies Code or the Statute and when authorized bodies or persons consider it advisable. The Supervisory Board may convene the Annual General Meeting if the Management Board fails to do so on time and may convene an Extraordinary General Meeting if it considers this advisable. Shareholders representing at least one-half of the Company share capital or at least one-half of total votes may also convene an Extraordinary General Meeting and appoint its chair. Shareholder or shareholders representing at least one-twentieth of share capital may demand that an Extraordinary General Meeting be convened and that specified matters be placed on its agenda; the demand must be submitted to the Management Board in writing or electronically. Dividend payment follows public-company rules. A dividend resolution should specify the dividend record date and payment date; subject to the KDPW Rules, the record date may be the date of the profit distribution resolution or a date within the following three months. The dividend is paid on the date specified in the General Meeting resolution, or, if the resolution does not specify a date, on the date set by the Supervisory Board. The Annual General Meeting also sets the dividend record date and payment date. The amount distributed to shareholders may not exceed profit for the last financial year plus retained earnings from previous years and amounts transferred from supplementary and reserve capitals created from profit and available for dividend, less uncovered losses, treasury shares, and amounts that under the Commercial Companies Code or the Statute must be allocated from last-year profit to supplementary or reserve capital. The Management Board may pay an interim dividend toward the expected year-end dividend if the Company has sufficient funds; the payment requires Supervisory Board consent. An interim dividend is permitted if the approved financial statements for the previous financial year show profit and may not exceed one-half of profit earned since the end of the previous financial year, shown in auditor-reviewed financial statements, increased by reserve capitals created from profit and available to the Management Board for interim-dividend purposes, and reduced by uncovered losses and treasury shares. The right to dividend belongs to persons whose securities accounts hold dematerialized bearer shares on the dividend record date and to entities entitled under dematerialized shares recorded in omnibus accounts. A shareholder claim against the Company for dividend payment may be exercised for 10 years from the date on which the Annual General Meeting adopted the resolution allocating all or part of Company profit for distribution to shareholders; after that period, the Company may refuse payment by invoking limitation. Shareholders have pre-emptive rights to subscribe for newly issued Company shares in proportion to their existing shareholdings, and this priority right also applies to securities convertible into Company shares or incorporating subscription rights for Company shares. A share capital increase resolution should state the date determining shareholders entitled to pre-emptive rights; this date may not be later than six months from adoption of the resolution. The agenda of the General Meeting adopting a share capital increase resolution should specify the proposed pre-emptive-right date. Exclusion of shareholders from pre-emptive rights to newly issued shares may occur only in the Company interest and only if announced in the General Meeting agenda. A Company shareholder may participate in the General Meeting and exercise voting rights personally or through a proxy. A shareholder intending to participate through a proxy must grant the power of attorney in writing or electronically. The Company takes appropriate actions to identify the shareholder and proxy to verify the validity of an electronic power of attorney, with detailed verification procedures included in the General Meeting convening notice. Under the Statute, participation in the General Meeting using electronic communication is permitted when the convening notice states that shareholders may participate electronically; in that case, the Company must provide shareholders with electronic participation capability. Detailed rules for an electronically held General Meeting are determined by the Management Board, taking into account the Rules of the General Meeting, and announced on the Company website together with the convening notice. A shareholder with shares recorded in more than one securities account may appoint separate proxies to exercise rights attached to shares in each account. If the proxy is a Management Board member, Supervisory Board member, liquidator, Company employee, or member of governing bodies or employee of a Company subsidiary company or cooperative, the power of attorney may authorize representation at only one General Meeting.
