Dom Development S.A. Group identifies tax-law volatility, internal risk-management architecture, group-management complexity, human-capital dependency and geographic concentration as material internal risk areas. Poland's tax system is described as unstable: over the last 20 years since Poland joined the EU, nearly 21,000 pages of acts and regulations have been passed annually on average; while the total number of regulatory changes has slightly declined since 2017, tax-related changes show an upward trend. The tax-system revolution tightened rules and reporting obligations, complicating business operations through requirements and concepts including JPK, transfer pricing, the Split Payment Mechanism, the so-called white list, the Teleinformation System of the Clearing House (STIR) and Mandatory Disclosure Rules (MDR). Frequent amendments, inconsistency and lack of uniform tax-law interpretation create significant tax-environment risk for the Company and Group. Challenges by tax authorities, including based on interpretations issued by the Minister of Finance, against the Company's and Group's tax settlements due to interpretative divergences, changes or inconsistent application by different tax-administration bodies may result in relatively high penalties or other sanctions. The National e-Invoicing System (KSeF), implemented from 1 February 2026 for large companies with turnover above PLN 200m and from 1 April 2026 for other entrepreneurs, is viewed by many businesses as a significant burden because of high implementation costs, accounting-system adaptation, technical concerns, technical and organizational requirements, security issues, financial risk from penalties, deadlines and time pressure, despite the Ministry of Finance's announced target document-flow simplifications. Given the relatively long tax-liability limitation period, tax-risk estimation is particularly difficult, and materialization of any described risk could materially adversely affect the financial position or operating results of the Company and Group. The Group defines risk as the possibility of an event negatively affecting operations, financial results or reputation; its risk-management system aims at early identification and adequate management of material business risks. The system is based on the Internal Standard for Risk, Compliance, Audit and Quality Management - Dom 2030, linked to the Risk Management and Compliance Policy. Group-management risk is uncertainty affecting business objectives, mainly operational, financial, reputational and legal risks. Since 2016 the Group has materially expanded: it broadened geographic reach to Wroclaw, the Tricity and surrounding areas, and Krakow; expanded operations into general contracting for its own developments; and implemented business-diversification plans, reflected in a material increase in subsidiaries' share of the Group's operating and financial results. The Management Board undertook broad measures to optimize Group management and create systemic frameworks for further development, both organic and through potential acquisitions. Recent Group changes create challenges in effective Group management and preserving organizational flexibility needed for quick responses to a highly competitive economic environment. These risks are mitigated by implementation and enhancement of IT systems supporting management reporting and information flow, and by harmonization of procedures, business processes and internal administration rules supervised by relevant Management Board members within their competence areas. Human-capital risk is material because development projects are unique, long-horizon undertakings where know-how, sourced mainly from experienced personnel, is a key competitive advantage. Group managers have broad Polish real-estate-market experience in searching for, acquiring, financing, building, selling, marketing and managing development projects. Uncontrolled departure of any Management Board member or employee key to the Company and Group could adversely affect operations, financial position, results and development prospects. Personnel-risk mitigation includes analysis of recruitment and development needs, reviews of key positions, ongoing analysis of employment conditions and factors affecting proper functioning of the area, harmonization of employment policies, implementation of anti-mobbing and anti-discrimination policies, and promotion of values derived from Group codes of ethical conduct. The Group reports relatively low employee turnover and many long-tenured employees; the Management Board reduces key-personnel-loss risk through incentive programs covering all Group employees and development of managerial competences and expert knowledge among operating-level employees. Concentration risk arises because the Company has for many years been the undisputed leader of the Warsaw primary residential market. Warsaw is Poland's main economic and social center and one of its largest academic centers; its attractive, absorptive market draws new residents, making it the largest residential market with the highest price level among Polish cities. The Warsaw market also poses the greatest development-execution challenges, as housing supply is under strong pressure from shortages of investment land with regulated legal status and delays in planning work and administrative decisions. The Management Board mitigates Warsaw concentration risk through Group geographic diversification via subsidiaries operating in the Tricity and surrounding areas, Wroclaw and Krakow; consequently, the Group operates in Poland's four most attractive residential markets.
