XTB S.A. Group 2025 consolidated annual report excerpt identifies material ESG impacts in E1 Climate and S1 Own Workforce. Under E1 climate-change mitigation and energy, current climate impact on XTB Group operations may be visible in selected global office locations especially exposed to extreme weather events. XTB Group climate impact is linked to its core business model based on IT infrastructure, including servers, and business expansion, driving rising electricity consumption and potentially higher greenhouse gas emissions. Actual negative impact concerns server energy intensity during standard use; potential negative impact may arise from increasing operational emissions due to higher electricity consumption; potential positive impact may result from energy-consumption optimization. The IT infrastructure and energy-use impacts affect the environment and are connected with XTB Group business strategy and operating model through expansion and development of XTB activity and through ESG Strategy objectives and actions. As of publication of the Statement, no perceptible effects of the identified impact are recognized. Expected effects may include higher energy consumption and the possibility of occurrence or intensification of extreme weather events in some XTB Group office locations. The impact is identified over short-, medium- and long-term operating horizons. Actions comprise successive replacement of IT infrastructure after the end of its lifecycle with newer, more energy-efficient equipment, use of renewable energy where possible, and calculation of the full carbon footprint of operations to enable observation of XTB Group emissions trends over several years. Climate impact has standard priority and arises in own operations. Under S1 working conditions, XTB Group has a strong, direct, multidimensional impact on employment security, working time, adequate pay, dialogue, freedom of association, and work-life balance of its own workforce, defined as employees under employment contracts and collaborators under civil-law contracts. Potential negative impact may occur if employment conditions deteriorate; actual negative impact stems from wage differences between IT employees and other employees, reflecting industry characteristics; actual negative impact also concerns internal corporate communication not keeping pace with XTB Group dynamic growth. Potential negative impacts may arise from a possible reduction in the number of employee representatives and from insufficient regulations. Actual positive impact results from implemented policies, employee regulations, cyclical employee assessment, annual employee satisfaction surveys, work-life balance initiatives, flexible employment conditions, competitive employment terms, unified employee assessment and remuneration rules, implemented and developed communication tools, and actions of a selected group of employee representatives. XTB identifies material impact on its own workforce through flexible working time and working mode, competitive employment terms, unified assessment and remuneration principles for XTB Group employees, communication development through new tools responding to rapid Group growth, employee representatives, and work-life balance activities. These impacts are linked with XTB business strategy and ESG Strategy through business objectives for Group development and ESG Strategy objectives concerning unified remuneration rules across XTB Group and organization of employee policies. Positive effects may include higher employee satisfaction, stronger organizational culture, stronger employer position, and talent acquisition from the labour market; negative effects may cause talent loss, higher turnover, and outflow of knowledge, experience, and know-how from the organization. Workforce impact is identified over short-, medium- and long-term operating horizons.
